## TOPIC 1 — Instagram wants 90-minute videos and is reportedly paying YouTubers to post them

### What Happened
Instagram started testing a long-form horizontal video format called "Features" on October 26, 2026, with videos up to 90 minutes. Business Insider reported that Meta has offered some YouTubers between $50,000 and $500,000 to post there, and Meta has confirmed it is funding some Features content. Reports say the deals don't require leaving YouTube. The $500K figure comes from a single Business Insider report, and Instagram hasn't said when it reaches everyone.

### Why It Matters
If Instagram turns into a long-form platform, where you post and who you compete with changes. Right now Instagram pays essentially nothing on long-form views unless you bring your own brand deals.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a creator across short-form platforms with Instagram as my portfolio. This is a platform shift affecting where small creators put their time.

### Hook
Instagram is literally paying YouTubers up to half a million dollars to post long videos, and I think I know why.

### Talking Points
- Instagram is testing videos up to 90 minutes, called Features, started Oct 26.
- Reportedly $50K to $500K offers to selected YouTubers (single Business Insider report), no YouTube exclusivity.
- Little to no ad revenue on it yet, and Instagram tried this before with IGTV, which shut down.

### My Angle
As a small creator with no offer coming, I won't chase it. Instagram stays a portfolio for me, and I'd wait to see if there's real ad revenue before spending effort on long-form there.

### 30–60 Second Script
Okay, so Instagram just started testing videos up to 90 minutes long. It's called Features, and it began on October 26th. And here's the wild part: Business Insider reports Meta has offered some YouTubers anywhere from fifty thousand to five hundred thousand dollars to post there. Meta has confirmed it's funding some of this content, but the deal terms are private, so take that half-million number with a grain of salt. Now, remember IGTV? Instagram tried long video in 2018 and shut it down. And right now there's basically no ad revenue on these long videos. So for a small creator like me, would I rush in? No. I'm keeping Instagram for my photography and events, and I'll watch to see if they actually pay regular creators. If you're a YouTuber, though, this is leverage. Are you going to post there? Tell me below.

## TOPIC 2 — Canon says camera prices could go up in 2027 because of AI

### What Happened
On its Q3 2026 earnings call, Canon said it no longer expects memory prices to fall in 2027. It said that if memory costs stay high, it will raise camera prices to offset them. Canon hasn't announced specific price changes, and it has a buffer from stockpiled memory. Fujifilm and Blackmagic Design have already raised prices.

### Why It Matters
AI data-center demand is pushing up memory costs, and cameras use a lot of memory (burst shooting, pre-capture, subject tracking). If you're planning a camera upgrade, waiting could cost more.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a pre-revenue photographer budgeting for gear. This hits my decisions directly.

### Hook
The AI boom might make your next camera more expensive, and Canon just said it out loud.

### Talking Points
- Canon no longer expects memory prices to drop in 2027 and will raise prices if costs stay high (conditional, nothing announced).
- AI data centers compete with camera makers for memory supply.
- Fujifilm and Blackmagic have already raised prices.

### My Angle
Buy gear for the work you have booked, not the hype. For a beginner business, the best move may be investing in what earns money (lenses, lighting, audio) rather than panic-buying a body.

### 30–60 Second Script
So Canon just warned that camera prices could go up in 2027, and the reason is AI. On their earnings call they said they no longer expect memory prices to come down next year, and if costs stay high, they'll raise prices to cover it. To be clear, nothing's been announced, and Canon says it has memory stockpiled for now. But Fujifilm and Blackmagic have already raised some prices. Cameras need a lot of memory for fast burst shooting and subject tracking, and AI data centers are competing for the same supply. As someone building a photography business, my take is: don't panic-buy. Buy what will actually get you paid. Are you upgrading soon or waiting? Let me know.

## TOPIC 1 — Instagram Just Launched 90-Minute Videos ("Features")

### What Happened
Instagram announced a new format called "Features" that lets select US creators post horizontal videos up to 90 minutes long. It was tested in Brazil first, and shorter clips from these videos show up in Reels. Instagram head Adam Mosseri has said it's "very much about TV". It's still limited to select creators, and TheWrap notes there's no YouTube-style revenue sharing on it.

### Why It Matters
Instagram has tried long-form before (IGTV, shut down in 2022). Now it's going after YouTube viewing time on TVs. If it opens up, creators get another place for long content, but there's no direct payout yet.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a creator who posts short-form on TikTok, Facebook and Shorts, and keeps Instagram for my portfolio. Whether long-form on Instagram is worth my time is a real decision for me.

### Hook
Instagram just said you can post 90-minute videos. Didn't they already try this and kill it?

### Talking Points
- "Features": horizontal videos up to 90 minutes, select US creators only for now, tested in Brazil first
- Clips from the long videos show up in Reels, so long and short feed each other
- Instagram tried this with IGTV in 2018 and shut it down in 2022; this time the pitch is TV screens, and there's no revenue share

### My Angle
As a small creator with no clients yet, I'm not rushing to make 90-minute videos. A short talking-head clip that points to a longer piece (event coverage, a behind-the-scenes) is more realistic. I'd wait to see if it actually pays or gets reach before changing my workflow.

### 30–60 Second Script
Instagram just announced something called Features, and it lets some creators post videos up to 90 minutes long. Ninety. Minutes. On Instagram.

Right now it's only for select creators in the US. They tested it in Brazil first. And clips from the long videos can show up in Reels.

Now, if you remember IGTV, Instagram already tried long videos once. They shut it down in 2022. This time the head of Instagram, Adam Mosseri, is saying it's really about TV, getting on the big screen where people already watch YouTube.

One thing though. Reporting says there's no revenue sharing like YouTube has. So you'd be posting long videos with no direct payout.

For me, I'm not changing anything yet. I'd rather make short clips that lead into something longer, like event coverage. But I'm watching this. If it opens up to everyone, would you post long-form on Instagram? Tell me below.

## TOPIC 2 — Nikon's $14,000 Lens With a Built-In Teleconverter

### What Happened
On October 29, Nikon officially announced the NIKKOR Z 120-300mm f/2.8 TC VR S, a pro sports zoom with a built-in 1.4x teleconverter. Flip it on and you reach up to 420mm at f/4. The US price is $13,999.95 and it ships in mid-November 2026.

### Why It Matters
It's a big-money lens aimed at pro sports and wildlife shooters. It shows where pro lenses are going: fewer lens swaps, more reach in one body of glass.

### Why It Suits Me
I cover events in Toronto and I'm a photographer. Gear talk is core to my niche, and I can give the honest "what would a small creator do" view.

### Hook
Nikon just announced a lens that costs almost fourteen thousand dollars. Here's who it's actually for.

### Talking Points
- 120-300mm f/2.8 with a built-in 1.4x teleconverter, up to 420mm f/4
- US price $13,999.95, ships mid-November
- It's built for pro sports and action work, not most of us

### My Angle
Big gear doesn't land clients. Most event work I do could be done with a lens costing a fraction of this. The lens is cool, but skill, speed and delivery matter more when you're starting out.

### 30–60 Second Script
Nikon just announced a lens that costs almost fourteen thousand dollars. Yes, really.

It's the 120-300 f/2.8 for Z mount, and the trick is a built-in teleconverter. Flip a switch and you get up to 420 millimeters at f/4, without swapping anything.

It's $13,999.95 in the US and ships in mid-November.

Is it impressive? Yeah. For sports and wildlife pros who need that reach and can't stop to change gear, I get it.

But for most of us, especially if you're just starting out like me, this isn't what gets you clients. Good photos, fast delivery and showing up matter way more than owning the biggest lens.

Would you ever spend this much on one lens? Let me know.

## TOPIC 1 — Google's Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash: 15-second AI video, cheaper, editable by chat

### What Happened
On Nov 5, 2026 Google released Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash. Clips can now run up to 15 seconds (up from 10), the 720p price dropped 30% to $0.07 per second, and you can edit a clip over several chat turns (e.g. swap a background or product) without regenerating it. It's available through the Gemini API/AI Studio, Flow and Google Vids, and rolling out in the Gemini app for Google AI Ultra subscribers. Google admits it still struggles with fast action and complex physics.

### Why It Matters
AI video is getting cheap and long enough for B-roll, product shots and ad clips. Clients may start asking "can't AI just do this?", and creators can use it for cutaways instead of paying for stock.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a videographer/photographer learning AI tools. Talking honestly about where AI video helps and where real footage still wins fits Pixel With Bliss.

### Hook
Google just made AI video clips longer and cheaper, so should videographers be worried?

### Talking Points
- 15-second clips (was 10), 720p priced at $0.07/sec, 30% cheaper
- You can edit a clip by chatting with it instead of regenerating
- Google itself says fast action and complex physics are still weak

### My Angle
AI is fine for filler B-roll, but clients hire me for real people, real events, real moments. Use it as a tool, not a replacement, and be upfront about it. SynthID watermarks mean AI clips can be identified.

### 30–60 Second Script
Google just dropped a new version of its AI video model, Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash, and it's a big deal for people like us. Clips can now be up to 15 seconds, up from 10. The price for 720p dropped 30 percent, to about 7 cents a second. And the part I find interesting: you can edit a clip just by chatting with it. Like, change the background without starting over. Now, Google itself says it still struggles with fast action and complex physics, so it's not magic. Here's my take. For quick B-roll or a product cutaway, this is going to be really useful. But nobody hires me for B-roll. They hire me to be at their event, capturing real people in real moments. AI can't be there. So I'm going to learn it and use it as a tool, not fear it. Are you going to try it? Let me know.

## TOPIC 2 — OM System says it seriously considered going full-frame

### What Happened
In an interview reported in early November 2026, OM Digital Solutions executives Setsuya Kataoka and Eiji Shirota said OM System did seriously consider full-frame or APS-C but decided against it and stayed with Micro Four Thirds. They described it as a trade-off: Micro Four Thirds for portability, full-frame for maximum image quality, and resolution is one of the biggest gaps.

### Why It Matters
It reopens the sensor-size debate: do most creators actually need full-frame? It also affects what gear people buy.

### Why It Suits Me
I shoot events and video, where size, weight and budget matter. As a pre-revenue creator, picking gear wisely is relatable.

### Hook
A camera company just admitted it almost went full-frame, and it chose not to.

### Talking Points
- OM System execs said they considered full-frame/APS-C but stayed Micro Four Thirds
- Their reasoning: portability vs ultimate image quality; resolution is the main gap
- Do you really need full-frame for events and short-form video?

### My Angle
For TikTok/Reels/Shorts, the viewer on a phone rarely sees the sensor difference. Skills, lighting and audio matter more. Full-frame wins in low light and for client work, so pick by use case, not hype.

### 30–60 Second Script
OM System, the company that took over Olympus cameras, just said something interesting in an interview. Their executives said they seriously thought about going full-frame, but they decided to stick with Micro Four Thirds. Their reasoning was basically a trade-off. Smaller format means lighter, more portable gear. Full-frame gives you the best image quality, and resolution is one of the biggest gaps. So here's my honest take as someone shooting events and video. Most of what we post is watched on a phone. Nobody scrolling TikTok can tell what sensor you used. Lighting, audio and storytelling matter way more. Full-frame does help in dark venues, and for client work I get why people want it. But don't blow your budget chasing it.

## TOPIC 1 — Instagram Is Testing 90-Minute Videos

### What Happened
On Monday, Oct 26, 2026, Instagram said it is testing a new upload type called "Features" that allows horizontal videos up to 90 minutes. A companion tool, "Clips as Reels", lets creators cut pieces of a long video into Reels that link back to the full video. The test starts in Brazil, with a small US rollout planned. Instagram has not announced how creators will get paid.

### Why It Matters
Instagram has been a short-form app. If long-form works there, creators may be able to keep their audience on one platform instead of sending them to YouTube. But there's no monetization yet, and Instagram dropped IGTV before.

### Why It Suits Me
I make talking-head content about platform changes and I'm a creator building an audience across platforms. Long-form on Instagram affects my own posting plans.

### Hook
Instagram just said it's testing 90-minute videos. Yes, Instagram.

### Talking Points
- Instagram is testing "Features": horizontal videos up to 90 minutes, starting in Brazil, small US rollout planned.
- "Clips as Reels" turns parts of a long video into Reels that point to the full video.
- No payout model announced yet, and Instagram already tried and shut down IGTV.

### My Angle
Don't move your whole strategy over yet. Treat it as a free test: Reels as the funnel, long-form as the depth. Without payouts, YouTube still pays creators from ad revenue. As a solo creator with a full-time job, I'd only repurpose what I already shoot, not create extra work.

### 30–60 Second Script
Instagram just said it's testing 90-minute videos. Yes, Instagram, the Reels app.

It's a new upload type called Features. Horizontal videos, up to 90 minutes. They're testing it in Brazil first, and there's a small rollout planned for the US.

There's also a tool called Clips as Reels. You cut parts of your long video into Reels, and those Reels link people to the full thing.

Now here's my take. Instagram's head, Adam Mosseri, said he used to be against long-form. Now he says they're committed to it long term. But there's no payout system yet. They haven't said how creators will earn from this. And remember IGTV? Instagram tried long video before and shut it down.

So I'm not rebuilding my plan around it. But if it reaches me, I'll test it with content I already have. Reels bring people in, the long video keeps them there. Would you watch a 90-minute video on Instagram? Tell me in the comments.

## TOPIC 2 — Nikon's New Lens Has a Teleconverter Built In

### What Happened
On Oct 29, 2026, Nikon published a press release announcing the NIKKOR Z 120-300mm f/2.8 TC VR S. Nikon calls it the world's first constant f/2.8 telephoto zoom with a built-in 1.4x teleconverter. Flip the switch and 300mm becomes 420mm. Nikon first said it was developing the lens in May 2026. Fstoppers reports a price of $13,999.95, about 6 lbs, and mid-November availability; I could not confirm price and date on Nikon's own page, so check before quoting them.

### Why It Matters
Sports and wildlife shooters normally swap teleconverters mid-shoot. A built-in one saves that. Pro gear is also getting very expensive, which raises the question of who this lens is for.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a photographer who covers events in Toronto and talks about gear. It's a good gear-reality-check video: cool tech, but is it relevant to a solo creator?

### Hook
Nikon just made a lens with a teleconverter built in, and it costs about as much as a used car.

### Talking Points
- 120-300mm f/2.8 with a built-in 1.4x teleconverter you switch on, so 420mm without swapping parts.
- Reported price about $14,000 and about 6 lbs, per Fstoppers (confirm with Nikon).
- Built for pro sports shooters. Most of us would rent or skip it.

### My Angle
Great engineering, but the real lesson for a growing creator: gear doesn't land clients, work does. If I ever needed this lens, I'd rent it for a single job and let the client pay for it.

### 30–60 Second Script
Nikon just announced a lens with a teleconverter built right in.

It's the 120-300 f/2.8. And you flip a switch and 300mm becomes 420mm, no swapping anything. Nikon says it's the first constant f/2.8 zoom to do this.

Now, it's made for pro sports shooters. Fstoppers is reporting around fourteen thousand dollars and about six pounds, so check Nikon for final numbers.

For me? I'm not buying that. I shoot events here in Toronto, and I'd rent it for one job and build it into the quote. Honestly, clients hire you for your work, not your gear list. But if you shoot sports or wildlife, this is the one to watch. Would you pay that much for a lens? Let me know.

## TOPIC 1 — Instagram Is Testing 90-Minute Videos ("Features")

### What Happened
On Monday, October 26, 2026, Instagram began rolling out "Features", a new upload type for horizontal videos up to 90 minutes, to selected creators in the US after testing in Brazil. Creators can cut clips from the full video and post them as Reels that link to the whole thing. It's mobile-app only for now, and Instagram says it's working on TV apps. No revenue share has been announced.

### Why It Matters
Instagram has pushed short-form for years and Adam Mosseri previously argued against long-form. Now it's going head to head with YouTube. Creators need to think about where their long content lives.

### Why It Suits Me
It's a platform change that affects every creator, and you post on Instagram with a portfolio focus. You can give a small-creator view on whether this matters.

### Hook
Instagram just said yes to 90-minute videos, after years of saying it never would.

### Talking Points
- Instagram "Features": horizontal videos up to 90 minutes, US test started Oct 26
- Reels clips can point viewers to the full video
- No payout model yet, unlike YouTube's ad revenue share

### My Angle
Don't move anything yet. It's a test for selected creators, with no pay. Keep long content on YouTube, but watch whether Reels-to-Features becomes a way to bring your portfolio audience into longer behind-the-scenes or event breakdowns.

### 30–60 Second Script
Instagram just did something it said it would never do. Starting October 26th, it's testing 90-minute videos. They call it Features. It's horizontal, up to an hour and a half, and right now it's only for selected creators in the US.

Here's the clever part. You can cut Reels from the long video, and those Reels link people to the full thing. So Reels become the trailer and Features becomes the movie.

But there's a catch. Instagram hasn't announced any way to get paid for it. YouTube gives you a share of ad revenue. Instagram says it's still exploring options.

So what do I think? Don't rebuild your whole strategy yet. It's a test. But if you shoot events or behind-the-scenes stuff, keep your raw footage. If this opens up, you'll want those long videos ready. Would you post long-form on Instagram? Tell me below.

## TOPIC 2 — Sigma fp II: Tiny Full-Frame, But No Built-In Viewfinder

### What Happened
Sigma officially unveiled the fp II (also written FP2), a pocket-size L-mount full-frame camera with a 24.5MP partially stacked sensor. Price is $2,199 body only, shipping November 11, 2026. It does 6K OpenGate video with L-Log, ProRes RAW support, and 70GB of internal storage. Despite rumors, there's no built-in EVF, and no IBIS. A tilting EVF-21 is a separate accessory.

### Why It Matters
It's a cinema-style camera aimed at video shooters, and the missing EVF and IBIS are the real talking points for anyone shooting handheld.

### Why It Suits Me
You're a camera and video creator who shoots cinematic work and events. This is gear news with a clear opinion angle: is it a cine tool or a photo camera?

### Hook
Sigma's tiny full-frame camera is finally official, and the rumor everyone believed was wrong.

### Talking Points
- $2,199 body, 24.5MP, 6K OpenGate, ships Nov 11
- Rumored built-in EVF is not there; EVF-21 is extra, and there's no IBIS
- Internal 70GB storage plus SD card slot

### My Angle
It's built like a cinema camera, not an everyday event camera. For handheld event coverage the lack of IBIS and a built-in viewfinder is a real compromise, so it's a B-cam or cinematic rig, not a one-camera solution.

### 30–60 Second Script
Sigma just made its tiny full-frame camera official, the fp II. And rumors had it wrong.

First the good stuff. It's 24.5 megapixels, shoots 6K OpenGate video, and has 70 gigs of internal storage on top of the SD slot. It's $2,199 for the body, shipping November 11th.

Now the catch. Everyone expected a built-in viewfinder. There isn't one. You can add one, but it's a separate accessory. And there's no in-body stabilization either.

So who's it for? To me it's a cinema camera in a tiny body. Great on a gimbal or a rig. But for handheld event coverage, I'd want stabilization. Would you buy it for video or photos? Let me know.

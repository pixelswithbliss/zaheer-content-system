## TOPIC 1 — Just Eat's AI Food Photos Went Wrong (and Got Paused)

### What Happened
Just Eat trialled an AI tool in the UK that generates menu photos for small independent restaurants who can't afford a photographer. Some results were bizarre, like a fried egg and sausages floating in a glass of cola. Just Eat apologised, removed the inaccurate images and paused the rollout while it develops the tool further. It has not dropped AI imagery altogether. Reports are dated around 21 October 2026.

### Why It Matters
Clients and platforms are testing AI as a cheap replacement for real photography. Where accuracy matters (food, products, real estate), fake images mislead customers. That's a real argument for hiring a photographer.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a photographer and content creator selling real photo and video work. Local businesses are exactly who I want as clients.

### Hook
Just Eat tried to replace food photographers with AI, and it put bacon in a glass of cola.

### Talking Points
- Just Eat's AI tool made menu photos for small restaurants that couldn't afford a photographer
- Some images were wrong (egg and sausages floating in cola), so Just Eat apologised, removed them and paused the rollout
- Paused is not cancelled, so the pressure on photographers isn't going away

### My Angle
AI is fine for ideas, but when the picture is the product, customers need the real thing. For small businesses, honest photos that look good are an edge, and that's what I offer in Toronto.

### 30–60 Second Script
Just Eat tried to replace food photographers with AI. And it went about as well as you'd think. They rolled out a tool in the UK that makes menu photos for small restaurants. Some of the results were wild. One had a fried egg and sausages floating in a glass of cola. Just Eat apologised, took the images down, and paused the rollout. But here's the thing. They paused it. They didn't cancel it. They still want to use AI images. So here's my take. If you run a restaurant, your photo is your promise. If I order something and it shows up looking nothing like the picture, I don't trust you. AI is great for ideas. But when the picture is the product, real photos win. If you're a photographer, this is your moment. Show restaurants that real, honest photos aren't a luxury, they're how you earn trust. Follow for more.

## TOPIC 2 — Sigma fp II: The Tiny Full-Frame Camera Is Back

### What Happened
Sigma officially announced the fp II on 23 October 2026. It's a very small full-frame camera with a 24.5MP sensor and 6K open gate video. It costs $2,199 USD body only and goes on sale 11 November 2026, per Sigma's US press release.

### Why It Matters
It's a small, modular camera built for filmmakers and hybrid shooters. Small bodies are good for run-and-gun event and cinematic work.

### Why It Suits Me
I shoot cinematic film and events and edit in DaVinci Resolve, so a small cinema-style full-frame body is directly relevant to my kit decisions.

### Hook
This is the smallest full-frame camera you can buy, and it shoots 6K open gate video.

### Talking Points
- 24.5MP full-frame sensor, 6K open gate video, $2,199 body only, on sale 11 November
- Open gate means you record the whole sensor, so you can crop to vertical and horizontal from one clip
- It's modular, so you build it up with accessories. Not for everyone, but great for filmmakers

### My Angle
Open gate is great for creators posting both horizontal and vertical. But a camera this small needs accessories to be comfortable, so the real cost is higher than $2,199. I'd want to see it at an event before I'd call it a main camera.

### 30–60 Second Script
Sigma just announced the fp II, and it's the smallest full-frame camera you can buy. It has a 24.5 megapixel sensor and shoots 6K open gate video. It's $2,199 for the body and comes out November 11th. Why do I care? Open gate means the camera records the whole sensor. So from one clip I can cut a horizontal video for YouTube and a vertical one for TikTok and Reels, without losing quality. For creators, that's huge. But there's a catch. The body is tiny, so to actually shoot comfortably you'll probably need a cage, a grip, maybe a monitor. That adds up fast. So is it my next camera? Not yet. I'd want to see how it holds up at a real event. But if you shoot cinematic stuff and want something light, it's worth watching. Would you shoot with a camera this small? Let me know.

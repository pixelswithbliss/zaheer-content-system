## TOPIC 1 — Sigma fp II: A Tiny Full-Frame Camera With 6K Open Gate

### What Happened
Sigma officially announced the fp II on October 22-23, 2026 (sources differ by a day). It is a pocket-sized modular full-frame camera with a 24.5MP partially stacked sensor, 6K open gate video and ProRes RAW out to an SSD. It costs $2,199 body only and ships November 11. There is no built-in viewfinder, despite the rumors; an optional EVF-21 is sold separately.

### Why It Matters
Open gate video lets you crop to vertical and horizontal from one clip, which suits creators posting to Shorts, Reels and TikTok. A small full-frame body is also easy to carry to events.

### Why It Suits Me
I cover cameras, shoot events and edit in DaVinci Resolve, so a tiny camera with RAW video is directly in my lane.

### Hook
This camera is tiny, shoots 6K open gate, and it still doesn't have a viewfinder built in.

### Talking Points
- 6K open gate and ProRes RAW in a very small body, $2,199 body only, ships Nov 11
- Reviewers note no in-body stabilization and no mechanical shutter, and accessories add up fast (the bundles are $2,749)
- Open gate means one clip can be reframed for vertical and horizontal

### My Angle
Does a creator actually need a cinema-style camera, or is it a gadget? As a solo shooter with a full-time job, I'd ask whether I'd really use RAW video, given the storage and grading time it adds in Resolve.

### 30–60 Second Script
Sigma just announced the fp II, and it's one of the smallest full-frame cameras you can buy. It shoots 6K open gate and can send ProRes RAW out to an SSD. Open gate is useful for us because you record the whole sensor and then crop to vertical or horizontal later. It's $2,199 for the body and ships November 11. But there are catches. There's no built-in viewfinder, so you'd add an external one. Reviewers also point out there's no in-body stabilization and no mechanical shutter. And once you add accessories, the price climbs fast. So is it for everyone? No. But if you shoot video, edit in Resolve, and want something tiny for events, it's worth a look. Would you buy it? Tell me in the comments.

## TOPIC 2 — Claude Duet: An AI That Works While You're Away

### What Happened
On October 21, 2026, Anthropic opened a research preview of Claude Duet, a standalone app where Claude acts as a proactive agent. It sends a morning brief, flags things in your inbox and calendar, and keeps working when you step away. It asks for approval before sending messages, paying, signing or deleting. Access is limited to US Pro and Max subscribers through a waitlist.

### Why It Matters
Solo creators lose a lot of time to admin like emails, bookings and follow-ups. An agent that handles some of that points to where AI tools are heading.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a solo operator working full time and building Pixel With Bliss on the side, and I'm learning AI automation. This is the same problem I'm trying to solve with automation.

### Hook
Anthropic just released an AI that works for you while you're asleep, and it asks before it does anything.

### Talking Points
- It's a research preview for US Pro and Max users on a waitlist, built on Opus 5.5
- It needs your approval before sending, paying, signing or deleting
- Anthropic itself says it can misjudge what's urgent

### My Angle
I would test it on client enquiries and booking follow-ups, not on anything involving money. I'd compare it with building my own automations in n8n, and I'd be honest that I haven't tried it yet (it's a waitlist).

### 30–60 Second Script
Anthropic just launched something called Claude Duet. It's a research preview, so not everyone can get it yet. It's for Pro and Max users in the US, through a waitlist. The idea is that Claude doesn't just wait for you to type. It gives you a morning brief, flags things in your inbox and calendar, and keeps working when you step away. The important part is that it checks with you before it acts, like sending a message, paying for something or deleting. Anthropic also admits it can get urgency wrong. As a one-person business, this is the kind of thing I care about, because admin eats my filming time. I haven't tried it yet, so I'm not going to pretend it's perfect. ## TOPIC 3 — Lightroom's New One-Tap AI Fix for Closed Eyes

### What Happened
Adobe announced Open Eyes for Lightroom mobile on October 19, 2026 (iOS, iPadOS and Android). When the app detects a blink, a one-tap icon appears, and generative AI replaces the closed eyes to fit the photo. Press reports say it can use other photos of the same person in your library for reference. It launched alongside Generative Expand, and some photographers have criticized Adobe's growing use of generative AI.

### Why It Matters
It makes blink fixes fast, but it also raises the question of where a retouch ends and a fake begins, especially for event and portrait work where clients expect real moments.

### Why It Suits Me
I shoot events and portraits as Pixel With Bliss, so group shots with someone blinking are a real problem I deal with.

### Hook
Adobe can now open the eyes in your photo with one tap, and photographers are split on whether that's okay.

### Talking Points
- One tap, generative AI, on Lightroom mobile; press reports say it can reference other photos of the same person
- Reports say free users get two uses (per DPReview; Adobe's post doesn't mention a cap), and the AI model used isn't confirmed by Adobe
- Debate: is this a normal retouch or changing the moment?

### My Angle
I'd say that culling well and shooting a burst still beat fixing it after, and that clients should be told when a face has been AI-edited. Where do you draw the line?

### 30–60 Second Script
Adobe just added a tool to Lightroom on mobile called Open Eyes. When someone blinks in your photo, you tap once and AI opens their eyes. It can reportedly look at other photos of that person to make it match. That's useful at events, where you get one great group shot and one person blinking. But photographers are divided. Is it just retouching, like removing a pimple? Or are you changing a real moment? Press reports say free users only get a couple of uses, though Adobe's own post doesn't mention a limit. My take: shoot bursts and cull properly first, and if I ever use AI on someone's face, I'd tell the client. Where do you draw the line? Tell me below.

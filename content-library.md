## TOPIC 1 — You can now edit photos with Photoshop and Lightroom inside Gemini

### What Happened
On September 24, 2026, Adobe began rolling out tools from Photoshop, Lightroom, Express and Firefly inside Google's Gemini. You ask Gemini in normal chat to fix lighting and colour, crop to a format, or batch-edit a set of photos. It's available across all Gemini plans, but you need an Adobe account. Adobe also added Acrobat tools to its existing Claude plugin the same day.

### Why It Matters
Editing is moving into the chat window. Photographers and creators can get consistent colour and social-ready crops without opening the apps, which changes how fast solo creators can turn work around.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a photographer who edits, makes content, and is learning AI automation. This is right where those three meet.

### Hook
You can now edit your photos in Lightroom just by chatting with Gemini.

### Talking Points
- Adobe's Photoshop, Lightroom, Express and Firefly tools now work inside Gemini, rolling out on all plans (Adobe account required)
- Use cases: consistent colour across a batch, lighting fixes, cropping for different social formats
- It's Adobe's "first step" with Google, and Claude already has an Adobe plugin, so this is a trend

### My Angle
As a solo creator who edits in DaVinci Resolve and Lightroom, I'd test it on a real event batch and be honest about whether it's faster than my own workflow. The debate: does this help small creators, or make everyone's photos look the same? Frame it as a tool, not a replacement for taste. (Only claim results after actually testing it.)

## TOPIC 2 — Nikon's new full-frame camera has no viewfinder

### What Happened
Nikon officially announced the Z5IIC on September 24, 2026. It's a 24.5MP full-frame camera based on the Z5II with in-body stabilization, but with no electronic viewfinder, so you frame using the rear vari-angle screen. It costs $1,399.95 body only ($1,699.95 with a 24-50mm kit) and ships mid-October. Video goes up to 4K 60p (60p is cropped to DX), with N-Log, H.265 10-bit and N-RAW.

### Why It Matters
A cheap full-frame with a flip screen and log video is aimed squarely at creators. It also raises the question of whether a camera without a viewfinder makes sense for photography.

### Why It Suits Me
I shoot video, photos and events, and I'm building a client business on a budget. Gear decisions are a big part of my audience's questions.

### Hook
Nikon just made a full-frame camera with no viewfinder, and I think it's for creators like us.

### Talking Points
- Z5IIC: 24.5MP full-frame, IBIS, 4K up to 60p (DX crop at 60p), N-Log, $1,399.95 body
- The trade-off: no EVF, only the rear 3.2-inch vari-angle screen, but it's lighter (537g vs 700g for Z5II)
- Positioned against compact bodies like Sony's a7C series

### My Angle
Ask: is a viewfinder optional in 2026? For talking-head and vlogging, the flip screen matters more. For event photography in bright Toronto sun, I'd miss the EVF. Give an honest "who should and shouldn't buy it" take. I haven't used it, so say that.

### 30–60 Second Script
Nikon just dropped a full-frame camera with no viewfinder. It's called the Z5IIC. Twenty-four megapixels, stabilization in the body, 4K video up to 60 frames, and N-Log. And it's fourteen hundred bucks for the body. What you don't get is an eye viewfinder. You shoot off the back screen, which flips out. That makes it lighter and smaller, so it's clearly aimed at creators and vlogging. But for me, doing events, in bright sun, I'd want that viewfinder. Now, 4K 60 is cropped, so keep that in mind. So who's it for? If you make videos and want full-frame on a budget, it's interesting. If you shoot photos all day outdoors, maybe grab the regular Z5 II. It ships mid-October. Would you buy a camera without a viewfinder? Tell me.

## TOPIC 1 — Lightroom Can Now Open Closed Eyes With One Tap

### What Happened
Adobe added an "Open Eyes" Quick Action to Lightroom on mobile. When Lightroom spots closed eyes in a portrait or group shot, a button appears. One tap generates new eyes that match the lighting and pose, using other frames from the same shoot as reference. Fstoppers reports it runs on partner AI models (the demo showed Google's Nano Banana 2), not Adobe Firefly.

### Why It Matters
It saves ruined group shots, but it generates eyes instead of copying real ones. It costs generative credits (Fstoppers: 20 for a phone file, 40 for a 24MP file, more on each retry). Fstoppers found results mixed: eyes opened, but eyebrows lifted on the first try. Digital Camera World reports some photographers are unhappy the app is now labelled an "AI Photo Editor" in the App Store.

### Why It Suits Me
I shoot events and portraits in Toronto. Closed eyes in group shots are a real problem for event coverage, and "is this still photography?" is a live debate my audience cares about.

### Hook
Adobe just added a button that opens people's eyes in your photos, and I have mixed feelings about it.

### Talking Points
- One tap on mobile, but the eyes are generated, not copied from another frame
- It costs AI credits, and big files from real cameras cost more
- Early tests are mixed: it can change the expression slightly

### My Angle
As an event shooter: great rescue tool, but it's not a substitute for taking more frames. And clients should know when a face has been generated. Where is your line between editing and faking?

### 30–60 Second Script
Adobe just added a button to Lightroom on your phone that opens closed eyes. One tap. You know that group shot where everyone looks great and one person is mid-blink? This is for that. But here's the thing. It's not borrowing eyes from another photo. It's generating new ones, and Fstoppers tested it and said the results are kind of hit and miss. In their test, the eyes opened, but the eyebrows went up and the guy looked surprised. It also costs AI credits, and bigger files from a real camera cost more than a phone photo. So for me as an event photographer? Useful emergency tool. But I'm still going to shoot extra frames, because a real frame beats a fake one. And honestly, if a client's face is being generated, should I tell them? Let me know where your line is.

## TOPIC 2 — Pixii, the Leica Rival, Is in Liquidation

### What Happened
Pixii, the French maker of screenless digital rangefinders with a Leica M mount, is in court-ordered liquidation. PetaPixel (Oct 20) and Imaging Resource, Digital Camera World and DPReview (Oct 22) covered it. Digital Camera World reports the court moved it from restructuring to liquidation on July 22, 2026, so the news is only now widely reported. Liquidation doesn't automatically mean the end; a buyer or investor could still rescue it.

### Why It Matters
Pixii was the one real alternative to Leica in digital rangefinders (Notebookcheck). Niche camera makers are fragile, and the next-generation model, expected around 2027, now looks unlikely.

### Why It Suits Me
It's camera gear news with a business lesson. As a solo creator building Pixel With Bliss, I can talk about betting on gear and brands that might not last.

### Hook
A camera company that dared to take on Leica just went into liquidation.

### Talking Points
- Pixii made a digital rangefinder with a Leica mount and no screen
- Court-ordered liquidation; a buyer could still save it
- Lesson: don't build your whole kit around a tiny brand

### My Angle
Most of us will never buy a Leica or a Pixii, but this shows why choosing gear with lenses and support that will last matters, especially when you're broke and building a business.

### 30–60 Second Script
Pixii, the little French company that made a digital rangefinder to take on Leica, is in liquidation. It was a weird camera. No screen on the back, and it used Leica lenses. For a while it was basically the only real Leica alternative. The court moved it into liquidation back in July, but the news only really blew up this week. There's still a chance somebody buys it or invests, so it's not totally over. But the next model probably isn't happening. My takeaway? When you're building a business with limited money, think hard before you build your kit around a tiny brand. Cool gear is great. Gear you can still get repaired in three years is better.

## TOPIC 1 — Luminar Now Runs in Your Browser (and There's a Free Version)

### What Happened
Skylum launched Luminar on Web around October 15–16, 2026. It's a browser version of its Luminar editor with RAW support, cloud sync, and AI tools like GenErase, Sky AI and Photo Restoration. The free tier has basic editing, 100MB of cloud storage and low-res exports (1280px). Paid plans are $69 (lifetime web and mobile access) and $159 (all platforms), and the generative AI tools on the bigger plan are a 365-day pass.

### Why It Matters
You can now edit RAW photos on any computer with just a browser, and quick edits no longer need a heavy install. The pricing is confusing, though, and the free version is limited.

### Why It Suits Me
It's a photo editing tool, so it fits a photographer and Pixel With Bliss owner. As someone who edits in DaVinci Resolve and posts fast, I can honestly compare it to my own workflow.

### Hook
Skylum just put a RAW photo editor in your browser, and the free version is real, but there's a catch.

### Talking Points
- Luminar on Web works in the browser with RAW support, no install needed.
- Free tier: basic tools, 100MB storage, 1280px exports. The AI tools sit behind paid plans.
- Pricing is confusing: some AI features are a 365-day pass, not lifetime.

### My Angle
Would I use it as a working photographer? It's handy for quick edits away from my main computer, but exports at 1280px on free mean it's not for client delivery. Be honest about who it's for: beginners and quick edits, not paid event work.

### 30–60 Second Script
Skylum just launched Luminar on Web. That means a RAW photo editor that runs right in your browser, no install. And yes, there's a free version. But here's the catch. The free tier only gives you basic tools, 100 megabytes of storage, and exports capped at 1280 pixels. That's fine for Instagram, but not for delivering to a client. The AI stuff, like erasing objects and restoring old photos, is behind the paid plans. There's a 69 dollar lifetime plan and a 159 dollar all-platform plan, and some of the AI tools on that one are only a 365-day pass. So it gets a bit confusing. My take? Great for quick edits when you're away from your main setup, or if you're just starting out. For paid event work, I'd still stick with my real workflow. Would you edit photos in a browser? Let me know.

## TOPIC 3 — Yashica's $119 Pocket Camera and the Cheap Digicam Wave

### What Happened
Around October 15, 2026, Yashica announced the Y16 Pocket Camera, a digital camera inspired by its 1959 film camera, with a US price of $119. Reports list a 12MP sensor, fixed 27mm-equivalent lens, built-in filters, 93g weight and seven colors. Preorders are open, with shipping planned from the week of November 30. Digital Camera World also reports that the Fujifilm Instax Pal 2 digicam became a No. 1 new release at a major photo retailer.

### Why It Matters
People are buying small, cheap, retro-style cameras for the look, not the specs. That says something about what audiences like right now.

### Why It Suits Me
It's a camera trend I can discuss as a photographer, and it connects to content: aesthetic and character can matter more than sharpness.

### Hook
A brand-new camera for $119 has a 12 megapixel sensor and a filter dial, and people are into it.

### Talking Points
- Yashica Y16: $119, 12MP, 27mm-equivalent lens, filters, seven colors, preorders now.
- Small retro digicams keep getting attention (the Instax Pal 2 was a top new release at one big retailer).
- Looks and feel are driving buying, not specs.

### My Angle
As someone who shoots cinematic work, I can say character beats perfection. A limited camera can be a creative tool. Challenge viewers: could you shoot a whole piece with a $119 camera?

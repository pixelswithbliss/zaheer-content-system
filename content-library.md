## TOPIC 1 — Hollywood's AI Line Just Got Crossed? David Fincher's "Cliff Booth" Backlash

### What Happened
David Fincher's new film *The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth* (starring Brad Pitt, a follow-up to *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*) is being criticized by critics and early viewers for what they call obvious generative-AI imagery. Filmmaker Patrick Tomasso called it the most "blatant and egregious AI use" he's seen in a studio film, and a "generative supervisor" is reportedly listed in the credits. In a Nov 19 LA Times interview, Fincher said "I'm not a Luddite" and asked "Should we not use it?" — but hasn't confirmed specifics. The film opens Nov 25 in IMAX and hits Netflix Dec 23.

### Why It Matters
When one of the most detail-obsessed directors alive gets called out for AI shots, it shows audiences CAN spot AI — and they care. It's a signal for every filmmaker and videographer about where viewers' trust line is.

### Why It Suits Me
I shoot cinematic film and edit in DaVinci Resolve. Talking about craft, AI tools and audience trust is exactly my lane — and it's a debate, not just news.

### Hook
"One of the most perfectionist directors in Hollywood just got called out for AI — and fans noticed before the movie even came out."

### Talking Points
- Critics who saw early screenings say shots look AI-made; a "generative supervisor" is in the credits. Netflix hasn't commented, and nothing is officially confirmed scene by scene.
- Fincher's response: "I'm not a Luddite… it exists, should we not use it?"
- The real issue isn't AI existing — it's AI that looks worse than what a crew would've shot.

### My Angle
Tool vs. shortcut. AI for cleanup, sky replacement or de-noising is fine — I use AI tools in Resolve too. But if the audience can SEE it and it breaks the moment, it failed as filmmaking. Ask viewers: where's your line?

## TOPIC 2 — TikTok Can Now Alert You If Someone Deepfakes Your Face (And Anyone Can Check AI Content)

### What Happened
On Nov 19 at its Safety and Innovation Forum in London, TikTok announced a global rollout of "Likeness Detection," an opt-in tool that alerts eligible creators and public figures when content or accounts may be using their face without consent. You verify your ID through a third party, review flagged posts, and report them. TikTok also launched a public AI-content checker — anyone, no account needed, can upload an image, video or audio file to see if it was made or edited with TikTok/ByteDance AI tools like CapCut or Dreamina.

### Why It Matters
Creators' faces are their brand. Deepfake protection going global is a big deal. But the checker only detects TikTok's own AI tools (via C2PA credentials and its VeraDura watermark) — it's not a general "is this AI?" detector, and credentials can be lost when files are re-shared or edited.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a face-on-camera creator who's learning AI tools — I can explain both the protection and the limits honestly, which most people won't.

### Hook
"TikTok will now tell you if someone is using your face in an AI video — but there's a catch most people are going to miss."

### Talking Points
- Likeness Detection is opt-in, needs ID verification, and is for "eligible" creators/public figures — not everyone yet; TikTok hasn't published a country-by-country timeline.
- The public checker works without an account, but only catches content from TikTok/ByteDance AI tools like CapCut and Dreamina.
- No result doesn't mean "real" — it might just be from another AI tool or had its metadata stripped.

### My Angle
Good step, but don't treat the checker as a lie detector. If you edit in CapCut, know that your AI edits can be traced. And if you're building a face-forward brand, opt in when you're eligible.

### 30–60 Second Script
TikTok will now tell you if someone is using your face in an AI video — but there's a catch most people are going to miss.

So TikTok just took its "Likeness Detection" tool global. It's opt-in. You verify your ID, and if TikTok finds posts or accounts that might be using your face without permission, it flags them so you can report them.

They also launched a public AI checker. Anyone — no account needed — can upload a photo, video or audio and see if it was made with TikTok's AI tools.

Here's the catch. That checker only spots content made with TikTok and ByteDance tools, like CapCut or Dreamina. If something was made with a different AI tool, or the file got re-saved and lost its data, it might come back clean. So "no result" does NOT mean "it's real."

Still — as someone whose face is the brand, I'm glad this exists. Would you opt in? Let me know.

## TOPIC 3 — Adobe Just Took Away Free Creative Cloud From Stock Photographers

### What Happened
Adobe has ended the free Creative Cloud plans it used to give Adobe Stock contributors. They're replaced by a "Redemption Code Program": 70% off Creative Cloud Pro for the first 500 contributors who hit 6,500 downloads and submit 1,000+ new images (or 10+ "datasets"), and 40% off for the first 10,000 with 2,500+ sales and 100+ new pieces. The qualifying window runs Sept 22, 2026 to March 12, 2027, with codes in April. Free access stays only for contributors Adobe commissions directly.

### Why It Matters
Back in 2020, 150 sales reportedly earned a free Photography plan. Now even big contributors get a discount, not free. Stock photographer Robert Kneschke called it a "clear step backward" and suggested the "datasets" criteria may be linked to AI training (his speculation, not confirmed by Adobe).

### Why It Suits Me
I'm building a photography business on the side — stock income and software costs are real decisions for photographers like me and my audience.

### Hook
"If you sell photos on Adobe Stock hoping for free Photoshop — that deal is basically gone."

### Talking Points
- Free Creative Cloud for stock contributors is replaced with capped discounts (70% / 40%) for high-volume sellers only.
- Thresholds jumped: 2020 was ~150 sales for a free photo plan; now 2,500+ sales for 40% off.
- Smart move for small creators: don't build your business around one platform's perks.

### My Angle
Stock isn't the side hustle it used to be. For someone like me starting out, local clients and event work pay way more than chasing 2,500 stock sales for a discount. Also a nudge to look at alternatives — I edit video in DaVinci Resolve, which has a free version.

### 30–60 Second Script
If you sell photos on Adobe Stock hoping for free Photoshop — that deal is basically gone.

Adobe used to give Stock contributors free Creative Cloud. Back in 2020, around 150 sales could get you a free photography plan. Now? It's discounts only. 70% off if you're one of the first 500 people with 6,500 downloads and over a thousand new images. Or 40% off if you're one of the first 10,000 with over 2,500 sales.

The only people still getting it free are contributors Adobe actually commissions.

One longtime stock photographer called it a clear step backward — and honestly, I get it.

My take as someone building a photo business from scratch: don't build around one platform's perks. Those can disappear overnight. Real clients, real shoots — that's what pays.

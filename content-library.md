## TOPIC 1 — SONY'S FIRST FISHEYE LENS IN 20 YEARS (FE 8-14mm f/3.5 G)

### What Happened
Sony officially announced the FE 8-14mm f/3.5 G on September 8, 2026 — its first-ever full-frame fisheye zoom for E-mount, and its first fisheye lens of any kind in 20 years. It zooms from an 8mm circular fisheye to a 14mm diagonal fisheye at a constant f/3.5, weighs about 314g, and uses dual linear-motor autofocus supporting 120fps tracking. It ships in October 2026 for $1,599.99 USD ($1,999.99 CAD), with preorders open now.

### Why It Matters
Fisheye lenses are a niche but powerful creative tool for event, wedding, and cinematic coverage, and a major brand like Sony re-entering the category after two decades signals renewed demand for creative wide-angle options — which could push more affordable third-party fisheye lenses into the market too.

### Why It Suits Me
As someone shooting events and cinematic content in Toronto, this is directly usable gear talk — I can speak to how a lens like this fits (or doesn't) into a working photographer's kit, from firsthand creative and budget perspective.

### Hook
"Sony just ended a 20-year drought — they finally made a fisheye lens again."

### Talking Points
- It's Sony's first full-frame fisheye zoom ever for E-mount, and their first fisheye of any kind in 20 years.
- It zooms from 8mm circular fisheye to 14mm diagonal fisheye — two distinct looks in one lens.
- It's compact (314g) but has pro-grade AF (dual linear motors, 120fps tracking) and even supports 3D VR pairing.

### My Angle
Fisheye lenses get dismissed as a gimmick, but one well-placed fisheye shot at a wedding or event can be the shot that stands out from every other vendor's coverage. Worth asking: is $1,600 worth owning outright, or is this a rental-only lens for that one hero shot?

It's called the FE 8 to 14 millimeter f/3.5 G, and it's actually their first full-frame fisheye zoom ever for Sony mirrorless cameras. What's cool is it zooms — at 8mm you get that full circular fisheye look, and at 14mm it switches to a more usable diagonal fisheye. It's tiny, under a pound, but it's got the same fast autofocus motors as their pro lenses. It ships in October for about sixteen hundred bucks. Now, as someone who shoots events and cinematic stuff, fisheye lenses get a bad reputation — people think they're just a gimmick. But one perfectly placed fisheye shot at a wedding or an event can be the shot everyone remembers. The real question is: is this worth owning outright, or is this a rental lens for that one hero shot? For me, at this price, I'd rent it before I'd buy it — but I'm curious what you all think."

## TOPIC 2 — OM SYSTEM REVIVES THE PEN CAMERA

### What Happened
OM Digital Solutions officially announced the OM SYSTEM PEN on September 9, 2026 — a 20-megapixel Micro Four Thirds interchangeable-lens camera reviving the classic rangefinder-style PEN line under the OM System brand. It features an EVF, a fully articulating screen, and IPX1 splash resistance (a first for the PEN line), launching alongside a new M.Zuiko 14-42mm III kit lens. It's priced around €1,089 / roughly $1,099 USD body-only, ships late October 2026, and is available for preorder now at B&H, Amazon, WEX, and Foto Erhardt.

### Why It Matters
It's a rare case of a major manufacturer reviving a beloved, discontinued camera line by demand, signaling renewed appetite for smaller, stylish cameras as an alternative to full-frame bulk — a trend also visible with Fujifilm's retro lineup.

### Why It Suits Me
This is a highly visual, nostalgia-driven gear story that plays well on short-form video, and lets me weigh in as both a working photographer and someone who cares about the aesthetic/creative side of gear, not just specs.

### Hook
"The camera everyone thought was dead just came back from the grave."

### Talking Points
- OM System just resurrected the PEN — a legendary compact rangefinder-style line that had been dormant for years.
- It's a 20MP Micro Four Thirds camera with a proper EVF, fully articulating screen, and splash resistance — a first for this line.
- It launches around $1,099 body-only in late October, aimed at creators who want a small, stylish everyday camera, not a spec monster.

### My Angle
The retro/compact camera trend keeps growing (Fujifilm's X100 line selling out, now this) because people want cameras that feel good to shoot with and look good on camera, not just more megapixels — worth questioning whether Micro Four Thirds can still compete when everyone else is chasing full-frame.

### 30–60 Second Script
"OM System just brought back a camera a lot of people thought was gone for good — the PEN. This is a twenty megapixel Micro Four Thirds camera with this gorgeous rangefinder-style body, a real electronic viewfinder, a flip-out screen, and it's actually splash resistant, which is a first for this camera line. It's launching in late October for about eleven hundred dollars body-only, and honestly it looks incredible — way more style than most cameras in this price range. Here's what I find interesting: everyone in the camera world is obsessed with full-frame and more megapixels, but brands keep bringing back small, good-looking cameras like this because that's actually what people want to shoot with and be seen with. Micro Four Thirds gets written off a lot, but a camera like this, in this price range, with this design — that's a real competitor for anyone who wants quality without carrying a brick around all day."

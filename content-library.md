## TOPIC 1 — Getty Images Is Going Private Through Bankruptcy

### What Happened
On Oct 12, 2026 Getty Images announced a plan to go private through a prepackaged Chapter 11 filing, backed by up to $533M from the Getty family and Koch Equity Development (Bloomberg reportedly counts new equity at up to $433M, with ~$100M of Koch debt converted). About $270M of unsecured notes would be retired, with most creditors on board. Getty says operations, vendors and contributors are unaffected. The formal filing had not yet been made as of the reports.

### Why It Matters
Getty is one of the biggest stock/editorial licensing platforms. Who owns it and how it licenses work in the AI era affects what photographers earn and how images get used.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a photographer building a business; stock, licensing and AI-vs-photographers is directly my world.

### Hook
Getty Images, one of the biggest photo companies in the world, is going bankrupt on purpose, and every photographer should understand why.

### Talking Points
- Getty plans a prepackaged Chapter 11 to go private with Getty family + Koch money (announced Oct 12).
- It follows the collapsed Shutterstock merger; Getty says contributors and customers aren't affected.
- Reports tie it to debt and legal costs; trade press speculates about AI licensing strategy (analysis, not confirmed).

### My Angle
Don't panic, but don't build your whole business on one platform. As a small photographer, own your client relationships and keep your own portfolio and licensing terms.

### 30–60 Second Script
Getty Images, one of the biggest photo companies on the planet, just announced it's going private through bankruptcy. Sounds scary, but here's what's actually happening. It's a prepackaged Chapter 11, so the deal is already lined up. The Getty family and Koch's investment arm are putting in up to 533 million dollars, and most of the creditors already agreed to the plan. Getty says contributors, customers and partners aren't affected, and business keeps running as normal. This comes after their big merger with Shutterstock fell apart earlier this year. So what does it mean for us? Honestly, nothing changes today. But it's a reminder that even giant photo platforms aren't safe, especially with AI changing how images get licensed. My take: don't rely on one platform. Build your own portfolio, your own clients, and know your licensing. That's exactly what I'm doing with Pixel With Bliss.

## TOPIC 1 — OPENAI SORA API SHUTS DOWN SEPTEMBER 24, 2026

### What Happened
OpenAI is discontinuing Sora entirely in two stages. The Sora app and website already went dark on April 26, 2026, and now the Sora API — the part developers and automation tools rely on — shuts down on September 24, 2026, three days after this brief. After that date, any videos or images still stored in Sora are permanently deleted.

### Why It Matters
Creators and small businesses who plugged Sora into their content pipelines (b-roll, concept mockups, social clips, or automations built in tools like n8n) lose access and their generated content for good if they don't back up and migrate before the deadline.

### Why It Suits Me
I talk about AI tools for creators and I'm building my own AI automation skills — this is a real, dated deadline my audience needs to know about, and it's a natural bridge into talking about which tools (Veo, Kling, Runway) actually replace Sora in a creator's workflow.

### Hook
"If you use OpenAI's Sora for anything, you only have 3 days left before it's gone for good."

### Talking Points
- OpenAI killed the Sora app back in April, and now the API shuts down September 24.
- After that, anything stored in Sora gets deleted — back up now.
- OpenAI says it's refocusing compute on coding tools and its "everything app," which tells you where AI money is actually going right now.

### My Angle
This is a lesson in not building your whole workflow around one AI tool, because these platforms can disappear fast. I'd point people toward stable alternatives instead of panicking.

### 30–60 Second Script
"Quick heads up if you use OpenAI's Sora for any of your content — you've got three days left. OpenAI already shut down the Sora app back in April, and now they're pulling the plug on the Sora API on September 24th. That means anything you generated and left sitting in there gets permanently deleted, and any automation or tool you built around it just stops working. Go export your stuff now if you haven't. Honestly, this is a bigger lesson for anyone using AI tools in their workflow — don't build your whole system around one platform, because it can disappear overnight. OpenAI's basically said they're putting their compute into coding tools and their all-in-one app instead. If you need a Sora replacement, Runway, Kling, and Google's Veo are the ones creators are switching to right now. Save your files, back up today."

## TOPIC 2 — CANON EOS R8 MARK II ANNOUNCED

### What Happened
Canon officially announced the EOS R8 Mark II on September 15, 2026 — a full-frame mirrorless camera adding in-body image stabilization (IBIS) and a second SD card slot to the original R8 platform, alongside a 24.2MP sensor, up to 40fps electronic-shutter shooting, and a notably deeper buffer.

### Why It Matters
The original R8 was already one of the most popular "affordable full-frame" cameras for creators stepping up from crop-sensor bodies. Adding IBIS and dual card slots fixes the two biggest complaints people had — it's now genuinely usable for paid event and video work, not just a stills-first hobbyist camera.

### Why It Suits Me
This is exactly the kind of hybrid stills/video camera that matters for someone shooting events and cinematic content — a real option for people trying to get into full-frame without spending R6 Mark III money, and my audience is at exactly that stage.

### Hook
"Canon just fixed the two biggest problems with one of their most popular cameras."

### Talking Points
- The EOS R8 Mark II adds in-body stabilization and a second SD card slot — both were the top complaints about the original R8.
- 24.2MP, up to 40fps with the electronic shutter, and a noticeably deeper buffer for RAW/JPEG bursts.
- Aimed at creators and event shooters moving into full-frame without jumping to R6 Mark III pricing.

### My Angle
I'd talk about whether this is actually the camera to recommend to someone starting out doing paid event work — the second card slot alone is a big deal for client trust, since backup redundancy matters more than people think until they lose a card mid-shoot.

### 30–60 Second Script
"Canon just announced the EOS R8 Mark II, and honestly, it fixes the two things everyone complained about on the original R8. First, it finally has in-body image stabilization, so you're not totally dependent on stabilized lenses anymore. Second — and this is the big one if you shoot for clients — it now has a second SD card slot. If you've ever had a card fail mid-shoot with no backup, you know why that matters. It's still a 24-megapixel full-frame sensor, up to 40 frames per second with the electronic shutter, and better buffer depth than before. This is really aimed at people like me — creators and event shooters who want full-frame quality without paying R6 Mark III money. If you're trying to move up from a crop-sensor camera into something you can actually use for paid work, this is worth putting on your list."

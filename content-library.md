## TOPIC 1 — Canva Just Cut 22% of Its Staff. What That Says About Creator Tools

### What Happened
On November 4, 2026, Canva announced a global restructuring affecting 1,271 people, about 22% of its workforce. CEO Melanie Perkins said growth "has slowed for the first time in a decade." Canva is merging its product, growth and platform teams, and it cut its revenue growth forecast from 30% to 20% because AI costs rose sharply. Reports say it faces pressure from chatbots like ChatGPT and Gemini that can make posters and presentations. Reports differ on how many of the 1,271 people actually lose their jobs; some may change roles or relocate.

### Why It Matters
Many creators and small businesses build their content on Canva. If AI chatbots are eating into design tools, the tools you rely on may change fast, in features, pricing or direction. No product or pricing changes were announced.

### Why It Suits Me
I make content for small businesses and creators. I use creator tools daily and I'm learning AI automation, so I can talk about which tools are worth depending on.

### Hook
Canva just laid off over a thousand people, and AI is a big part of the reason.

### Talking Points
- Canva is cutting about 22% of staff after growth slowed for the first time in a decade.
- AI costs went up, and chatbots now make designs that used to need Canva.
- Don't build your whole workflow on one tool.

### My Angle
Don't panic and don't gloat. The lesson for a small creator: own your skills (editing, storytelling, shooting) and keep your workflow portable between tools.

### 30–60 Second Script
Canva just laid off over a thousand people. About 22% of the company. And the CEO said growth slowed for the first time in a decade.

So what happened? Reports say AI costs went up a lot, and Canva cut its growth forecast from 30% to 20%. At the same time, tools like ChatGPT and Gemini can now make posters and presentations in seconds. That's competition Canva didn't have a few years ago.

Now, nothing has been announced about changes to Canva for users. So don't panic. But this is a good reminder for us as creators. Whatever tool you use, it can change. Prices change, features change, companies shrink.

So here's what I'm doing. I'm focusing on skills the tools can't replace, like shooting, editing and storytelling. And I'm keeping my projects easy to move between apps. Are you building on one tool, or spreading it out? Let me know.

## TOPIC 2 — ChatGPT Is Merging Chat and Work Into One

### What Happened
OpenAI emailed users that the Chat and Work tabs in the ChatGPT desktop app will combine into one experience, reported by TechCrunch on November 2, 2026. The combined version will let ChatGPT take an idea further, such as building websites or presentations and doing web tasks while you review. It goes to select users first. Codex stays separate but shares the same usage pool, so some users may hit usage limits more often. OpenAI's own email isn't published; this comes from TechCrunch's report of it.

### Why It Matters
If you use ChatGPT for captions, scripts, client emails or automations, the way you use it is changing. Shared limits mean heavy users could run out sooner.

### Why It Suits Me
I use AI for content planning and I'm learning automation. A simple "what changes for you" take is useful to other creators.

### Hook
ChatGPT is about to change how you use it, and it could affect your usage limits.

### Talking Points
- Chat and Work tabs are becoming one experience.
- It can build things like websites and presentations, not just answer questions.
- Codex shares the same usage pool, so limits may hit sooner for heavy users.

### My Angle
Fewer tabs is nice, but the real question is limits. Test it on one real task, like a client proposal or a content calendar, before trusting it.

### 30–60 Second Script
OpenAI is merging two parts of ChatGPT into one: Chat and Work.

Right now, they're separate tabs in the desktop app. OpenAI told users they'll combine into one place, where ChatGPT can take your idea further. Think building a website or a presentation, or doing tasks on the web while you check its work.

It's rolling out to select users first, so you might not see it yet. One thing to watch: Codex stays separate, but it shares the same usage pool. OpenAI says most people won't notice, but heavy users could wait longer for limits to reset.

For us creators, I'd say try it on one real task. Like a content calendar or a client proposal. Don't rebuild your whole workflow around it yet. Are you using ChatGPT for content? Tell me how.

## TOPIC 3 — TikTok's Must Eat: Creators Powering a Restaurant Guide (freshness: about 7 days old)

### What Happened
In late October 2026 TikTok launched Must Eat, an in-app restaurant guide built from more than 40 million posts and comments. It's available first in New York City, Los Angeles and the San Francisco Bay Area, with reservations at selected restaurants. TikTok says restaurants can't pay for placement. TechCrunch (Oct 31) frames it as part of TikTok's push to become a "super app." It is not available in Toronto, and no wider rollout date has been announced.

### Why It Matters
Creator videos are becoming TikTok's local search. Food and place content can now lead straight to a listing or booking. It's also a signal that TikTok wants to compete with Google Maps and Yelp.

### Why It Suits Me
Pixel With Bliss covers Toronto content and events. Local, location-tagged content is where this is heading, and restaurants are potential clients.

### Hook
TikTok is turning food videos into a restaurant guide, and it's built from creators' content.

### Talking Points
- Must Eat uses over 40 million posts and comments.
- Launched in NYC, LA and the Bay Area, not Toronto yet.
- Restaurants can't pay for placement, so creator content matters.

### My Angle
If it comes to Toronto, local creators who tag locations and shoot good food content could benefit. Restaurants will need creators like us.

### 30–60 Second Script
TikTok just launched its own restaurant guide, and it's built from creator videos.

It's called Must Eat. TikTok says it draws on over 40 million posts and comments, and you can filter by things like cuisine and rating. At some restaurants you can even reserve a table in the app.

Right now it's only in New York, Los Angeles and the Bay Area. Nothing yet for Toronto. And TikTok says restaurants can't pay to be listed.

So if this comes to our city, local content matters more. Tag your locations. Shoot good food and venue content. And if you run a restaurant, you'll want creators talking about you. I'm a Toronto creator, so I'm watching this closely. Would you use TikTok to pick where to eat?

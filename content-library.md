## TOPIC 1 — Instagram Now Lets Creators Put Amazon Affiliate Links Directly in Reels (US Only — For Now)

### What Happened
On November 16, 2026, Instagram launched a way for creators to tag Amazon products directly inside Reels and feed posts, with the affiliate link attached automatically. Creators can tag up to 30 products per Reel and 20 per feed post. Instagram says it takes no commission and charges no fees — Amazon still pays the creator's commission. Right now it's only for US creators, 18+, with a professional account who are in Amazon's Influencer program; Instagram hasn't said if it will expand to other countries.

### Why It Matters
Until now, affiliate links lived in your bio, Stories or DMs. Meta says product links placed directly on Reels and posts get about twice as many clicks as bio links (that's Meta's own number). For gear reviewers and tech creators, that's a direct path from "what camera do you use?" to a commission — without the "link in bio" drop-off.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a creator who talks about cameras, lenses and editing gear — exactly the kind of content people buy from. And as a Toronto-based creator, I can give the honest Canadian angle: this isn't available to us yet, so here's what to do in the meantime.

### Hook
"Instagram just made it way easier for creators to get paid for recommending gear — but if you're in Canada like me, there's a catch."

### Talking Points
- What launched: Amazon affiliate links right inside Reels (up to 30 products) and posts (up to 20), with no cut taken by Instagram.
- Why it's big: Meta says links on the post get about 2x the clicks of a bio link — less friction between "nice camera" and "buy."
- The catch: US creators only for now, and you still need to disclose that you earn a commission — a product tag doesn't do that for you.

### My Angle
Monetization features keep launching US-first, and Canadian creators get left waiting. My take: don't wait on Instagram — start building your "gear I actually use" list now, and be upfront about affiliate links. Trust is worth more than a quick commission. Also worth asking: does this push Reels even further toward being a shopping channel?

## TOPIC 2 — Photographers Are "Vibe-Coding" Their Cameras With AI — And Now a Camera Company Is Inviting It

### What Happened
Photographers have been using AI coding tools to build custom firmware for older cameras — for example, projects that add film-style looks to old Canon DSLRs (building on Magic Lantern) or new film simulations to a Fujifilm X70. On November 16, 2026, PetaPixel reported that Camera Intelligence, maker of the AI-focused Caira Micro Four Thirds camera, is opening its camera OS with a "Creative Sandbox" where users can build their own camera features using their own AI coding tools. Not everyone is a fan: Fuji X Weekly's Ritchie Roesch argued on Nov 7 that AI-written third-party firmware is risky and could brick cameras.

### Why It Matters
This is the first time "AI coding" and "camera features" have collided this directly. Instead of waiting for a firmware update from Canon or Sony, photographers are starting to make their own. That could mean more creative freedom — or a lot of dead cameras and voided warranties.

### Why It Suits Me
It's the exact overlap of my two worlds: photography and learning AI automation. I can talk about it as someone who uses AI tools to build workflows, and as someone who relies on my camera for paid client work.

### Hook
"Photographers are now using AI to rewrite the software inside their cameras — and one camera company just said, 'go ahead.'"

### Talking Points
- The trend: people are using AI coding tools to build custom firmware — like film looks on 20-year-old DSLRs.
- The new development: Camera Intelligence is opening its Caira camera with a "Creative Sandbox" so users can build their own features.
- The debate: critics say AI-written firmware could brick cameras, shorten their life, and hurt resale value.

### My Angle
As someone learning AI automation, I love the idea — but I'd never run vibe-coded firmware on the camera I use for client shoots. My rule: experiment on an old, cheap body you're okay losing; keep your work camera on official firmware. And the bigger question: if camera brands don't open up, will users just do it for them?

## TOPIC 3 — Facebook Is Deleting Extra Profiles on December 14 — Check Yours Now

### What Happened
On November 12, 2026, Meta updated its 2023 announcement about multiple personal profiles: Facebook is removing "additional profiles" — the secondary profiles people could create alongside their main one. Meta says these will be permanently removed after December 14, 2026. Your main profile and your Facebook Pages are not affected. Meta says affected users are being notified by email and in-app.

### Why It Matters
Some creators and small businesses set up an extra profile to separate work from personal life, or to run groups. If that's you, anything on that profile is on a countdown. Coverage notes that groups managed only by an additional profile could be left without admins.

### Why It Suits Me
I run Pixel With Bliss on Facebook alongside my other platforms, and lots of local creators and small businesses I want to work with rely on Facebook. A practical "protect your account" tip builds trust and shows I'm on top of platform changes.

### Hook
"If you made a second Facebook profile for your business or side hustle — you've got until December 14th before it's gone for good."

### Talking Points
- What's happening: Facebook is removing "additional profiles" after Dec 14, 2026 (Meta's official update, Nov 12).
- What's safe: your main profile and your Facebook Pages aren't affected.
- What to do now: download anything you need, move group admin rights to your main profile, and make sure your business lives on a Page.

### My Angle
This is a reminder that you don't own your platform. If your business lives on a feature Meta can switch off, you're renting. My take: use a proper Facebook Page for your business, keep backups of your work, and always have a home base you control — a website or email list.

### 30–60 Second Script
If you made a second Facebook profile for your business or side hustle — you've got until December 14th before it's gone for good.

So a few years ago, Facebook let you create extra profiles under your main account. Lots of people used them to separate personal stuff from work, or to run groups.

Well, Meta just confirmed those additional profiles will be permanently removed after December 14th, 2026. Your main profile is fine. Your Facebook Pages are fine. It's just those extra profiles.

So here's what I'd do this week. One — download anything on that profile you want to keep. Two — if it's an admin on any groups, add your main profile as an admin. And three — if your business was running off that profile, move it to a proper Facebook Page.

And honestly? Bigger lesson. Platforms change the rules all the time. Always have a backup of your work, and a home base you own.

Share this with someone who needs to hear it.

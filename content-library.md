## TOPIC 1 — Instagram Is Going Long-Form: 90-Minute "Features" Test

### What Happened
On October 26, 2026, Instagram announced a test of "Features", horizontal videos that can run up to 90 minutes. It started in Brazil and is expanding to a limited group of US creators. Creators can also turn parts of a long video into Reels that link back to the full video. Instagram has not announced any ad revenue share, and Meta says it's too early to share a long-term monetization model.

### Why It Matters
Instagram has pushed short vertical video for years, and now it's chasing YouTube-style long-form. If it works, creators may get another place for longer content, plus a way to funnel Reels viewers to it. Without ad revenue, the payoff is unclear for now.

### Why It Suits Me
It's a platform change that affects every creator I'm talking to, and I run Instagram as a photography/event portfolio. It also raises the question of whether long-form belongs on a portfolio account at all.

### Hook
Instagram just said it will let you upload 90-minute videos. Yes, Instagram.

### Talking Points
- Instagram is testing "Features": horizontal videos up to 90 minutes, announced Oct 26. Limited test, Brazil first, small US group now.
- Reels can link back to the full video, so short clips become a funnel to long-form.
- No ad revenue share yet. YouTube pays creators 55% of ad revenue, so money is the big question.

### My Angle
I'm a solo creator with a day job, so I can't make everything for every platform. Long-form is a time sink with no pay announced. My take: don't rush; keep Reels and photography on Instagram, and watch the test until there is a monetization model. For a photographer, a behind-the-scenes event breakdown could be a good fit if the feature opens up.

### 30–60 Second Script
Instagram just announced something I didn't expect. They're testing 90-minute videos. They're calling it "Features", and it was announced on October 26. So the app that taught everyone to watch 15-second clips now wants your podcasts, your vlogs, your full interviews.

Right now it's only a test. It started in Brazil, and a small group of creators in the US are getting it. You can also cut a Reel from the long video and link it back to the full thing. Basically, Reels become the trailer.

Here's the catch. Instagram hasn't said how you get paid. Meta says it's too early. Compare that to YouTube, where creators get 55% of ad revenue.

So what do I think? If you're a small creator like me, don't drop everything and start making hour-long videos. Keep doing what's working, and watch how this test plays out. If there's money in it later, you'll be glad you waited and saw how it went. Would you post long videos on Instagram? Tell me in the comments.

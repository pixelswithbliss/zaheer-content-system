## TOPIC 1 — Sigma FP2: The Tiny Full-Frame Camera Is Back

### What Happened
On October 23, 2026, Sigma officially announced the FP2, a follow-up to its tiny modular full-frame camera. It has a 24.5MP partially stacked sensor, 6K OpenGate video, ProRes RAW and L-Log, and up to about 20fps burst. It costs $2,199 body-only and goes on sale November 11, 2026. Sigma calls it the world's smallest full-frame body, a claim it limits to "as of October 2026, by Sigma."

### Why It Matters
It's a full-frame body that mixes real cinema features with a stills mode, built around add-ons (EVF, base plate, hot shoe unit) so you only pay for the size and weight you need. For solo shooters, that's a different idea from the usual big hybrid body.

### Why It Suits Me
I shoot events and cinematic video as a one-person operation. A small body with a still/cine switch and internal storage is exactly the kind of gear I'd think about for run-and-gun work.

### Hook
Sigma just made a full-frame cinema camera that's smaller than most lenses I've held.

### Talking Points
- Sigma announced the FP2 on Oct 23: $2,199 body-only, on sale Nov 11, L-mount.
- Specs: 24.5MP partially stacked sensor, 6K OpenGate, ProRes RAW, about 20fps burst, 70GB internal storage.
- It's modular. The EVF, base plate and hot shoe are extra, so the real cost depends on your setup.

### My Angle
Spec sheets are easy to read out. I can talk about what a solo event shooter actually needs: is small better when you have to add a cage, EVF and audio anyway? I haven't used it, so I'd frame this as a reaction, not a review.

### 30–60 Second Script
Sigma just announced the FP2, and it's one of the most interesting cameras I've seen this year. It's a full-frame camera, and Sigma says it's the smallest one in the world right now. Twenty-four megapixels, 6K open gate video, ProRes RAW, and around twenty frames a second for stills. It's $2,199 for just the body, and it ships November 11th. But here's the catch. It's modular. The viewfinder, the base plate, the hot shoe, those are all extra. So that tiny body can get big and expensive fast. For someone like me, shooting events and cinematic stuff on my own, I like the idea of a camera that's small when I need it small. I haven't touched one yet, so I'm not going to tell you it's perfect. But if you want a cinema camera that can also shoot stills, this is one to watch. Would you buy it, or stick with a normal hybrid body? Tell me in the comments.

## TOPIC 2 — Claude Duet: An AI Agent That Works While You Sleep

### What Happened
On October 21, 2026, Anthropic announced Claude Duet, a research preview of a proactive AI agent app. It sends a morning brief, checks your connected apps every few hours, and does tasks in the background on its own virtual machine. It asks for your approval before it sends a message, makes a payment, signs something, or deletes anything. Access is by waitlist for US Pro and Max subscribers only.

### Why It Matters
Most AI tools wait for you to open them. This one is built to work when you're not there, which could take on the admin side of running a creator business: inbox, scheduling, invoices.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm learning AI automation and building Pixel With Bliss next to a full-time job, so time is the limit. Anything that handles admin while I'm at work is relevant, and it's a natural bridge to my n8n and automation content.

### Hook
Anthropic just launched an AI that keeps working after you close the app.

### Talking Points
- Duet sends a morning brief, checks your apps every few hours, and runs tasks on its own virtual machine.
- It must get your approval before sending messages, paying, signing or deleting.
- It's a research preview, US-only, Pro and Max waitlist. Background activity counts toward your usage limits.

### My Angle
I'm not going to hype this. I'm a Canadian solo creator who can't get on the waitlist yet, so I can ask the real question: what would I hand off first, and what would I never let an agent touch? Compare it with building my own automations in n8n, where I control every step.

### 30–60 Second Script
Anthropic just launched something called Claude Duet, and it's not like the chatbots you're used to. Normally, you open the app and ask it something. Duet works the other way around. It sends you a morning brief, checks your apps every few hours, and does tasks in the background, even when your phone is off. The important part is that it has to ask you before it sends a message, pays for something, signs a document, or deletes anything. Right now it's a research preview, it's a waitlist, and it's only for Pro and Max users in the US, so I can't even try it yet. And Anthropic admits it can misread things. But as a creator with a full-time job on the side, I keep thinking about what I'd hand off. Emails, scheduling, client follow-ups.

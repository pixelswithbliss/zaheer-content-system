## TOPIC 1 — Adobe Just Stopped Giving Stock Photographers Free Creative Cloud

### What Happened
Adobe no longer gives free Creative Cloud subscriptions to most photographers who sell on Adobe Stock. It swapped that perk for discount codes. According to PetaPixel, there's 70% off Creative Cloud Pro for the first 500 contributors who hit 6,500 downloads and upload 1,000+ new images, plus another 70% tier for contributors with 10+ "datasets". There's also 40% off for the first 10,000 contributors with 2,500+ sales and 100+ unique pieces. Free access stays only for contributors Adobe actively commissions.

### Why It Matters
Stock photography was already a low-paying side income, and the free software was one of the few real perks. A "dataset" tier makes people ask whether Adobe now values contributors mainly as AI training data. Anyone thinking of stock as a side income has to rethink the math.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a working photographer building a business on the side, so subscription costs and passive-income ideas like stock are things I actually weigh. It's an honest small-creator view, not a corporate one.

### Hook
"If you sell photos on Adobe Stock, Adobe just took away your free Photoshop."

### Talking Points
- The free Creative Cloud perk is gone for most contributors, and discount codes replace it. Most tiers are capped at the first 500 or 10,000 people.
- One tier rewards "datasets", and photographers are asking if that's about AI training.
- If you're just starting out, stock probably isn't the shortcut it used to be. Your own clients and portfolio matter more.

### My Angle
As someone building toward full-time, I'd say: don't build your business on another company's perks. Own your clients and your audience. Also, open question to viewers: is it fair to ask for "datasets" from photographers when AI is competing with them?

### 30–60 Second Script
If you sell photos on Adobe Stock, Adobe just took away your free Photoshop.

For years, if you sold enough on Adobe Stock, you could get Creative Cloud for free. That's basically over. Now it's discount codes. 70% off if you're one of the first 500 people to hit some pretty big numbers, like 6,500 downloads and over a thousand new images. Or 40% off for the first 10,000 people with 2,500-plus sales.

And here's the part people are side-eyeing. One of the 70% tiers is for contributors with ten or more "datasets." Some photographers think that's about AI training. Adobe hasn't explained it clearly.

Me personally? I'm building my photography business on the side, and this is a reminder. Don't build your plan on another company's perks. They can change the rules overnight. Build your own clients, your own portfolio, your own audience.

Are you still doing stock, or have you given up on it? Let me know.

## TOPIC 2 — Canva Now Has an "Off Switch" for AI

### What Happened
On Nov 19, Canva added a setting for Free and Pro users that hides its generative AI tools, including Magic Write, Magic Expand, Magic Design, Dream Lab and Canva Code. It's under Profile > Settings > Permissions > "Enable generative AI features". Background Remover and Magic Grab stay on, because Canva says they only work with what's already in your design. A second setting shows you less AI-generated content in the template and element library. It's rolling out gradually.

### Why It Matters
This is one of the biggest creative platforms admitting that a lot of its users don't want AI pushed on them. Creators who want human-made assets, or clients who don't want AI in their branding, now have an easy option. It may also push other apps to add the same kind of switch.

### Why It Suits Me
I use AI and I'm learning AI automation, but I'm also a photographer who sells real, human-made work. I can speak to both sides without sounding anti-tech or like an AI hype guy.

### Hook
"Canva just did something almost no tech company is doing right now: they gave you an off switch for AI."

### Talking Points
- Where the switch is and what it turns off. Background remover still works.
- A second setting shows less AI-generated content when you're browsing templates.
- My take: AI should be a tool you pick up on purpose, not something forced into every button.

### My Angle
I'm pro-AI for the boring stuff like automation, admin and resizing, but the creative part is what clients pay me for. Canva's move proves that "choice" is the real feature. Ask viewers: would you turn it off?

### 30–60 Second Script
Canva just did something almost no tech company is doing right now. They gave you an off switch for AI.

If you're on Canva Free or Pro, go to your Profile, Settings, Permissions, and there's a toggle called "Enable generative AI features." Turn it off and things like Magic Write, Magic Expand and Dream Lab get hidden. Background Remover and Magic Grab still work, which, honestly, are the ones I actually use.

There's also a second setting so you see less AI-generated stuff when you're browsing templates.

Now, I'm not anti-AI. I literally use it to automate parts of my business. But for the creative side, the part clients actually pay me for, I want to choose when AI shows up. Not have it in every single button.

So that's the real feature here. Choice. Would you turn it off, or keep it on? Tell me below.

## TOPIC 3 — You Can Finally Edit Instagram Stories After Posting (But Only for 15 Minutes)

### What Happened
On Nov 18, Instagram head Adam Mosseri announced that you can now edit a Story's text, music, stickers and drawings for up to 15 minutes after posting, without losing views, likes or replies. Edited Stories show an "Edited" label. It's under the three-dot menu on your Story, and it's rolling out gradually. The same update adds an easier multi-photo Story layout, better backgrounds for photos that don't fit, and new sticker designs.

### Why It Matters
A typo or the wrong song used to mean deleting the Story and losing its views. Now there's a short window to fix it. It's useful for anyone who posts behind-the-scenes or event Stories in real time.

### Why It Suits Me
I post event coverage and behind-the-scenes Stories for Pixel With Bliss, often fast and on the go. It's a practical, quick tip I can demo from my own phone.

### Hook
"Ever posted an Instagram Story with a typo and had to delete it? You don't have to anymore. But there's a catch."

### Talking Points
- You can now edit text, music, stickers and drawings, and you keep your views and replies.
- The catch is the 15-minute window. The edit option may also not show up on Stories shared to Facebook or ones with some special stickers.
- The multi-photo layout update is great for event recaps.

### My Angle
Practical creator tip: still proofread before posting, but this saves you on fast-paced shoot days. Show it on screen. Frame it as "the 15-minute rule" so it's memorable.

### 30–60 Second Script
Ever posted an Instagram Story with a typo and had to delete it? You don't have to anymore. But there's a catch.

Instagram just started rolling out Story editing. Tap the three dots on your Story, hit "Edit story," and you can change the text, the music, stickers, even drawings. And you keep your views, likes and replies. It'll just show a little "Edited" label.

The catch? You've only got 15 minutes. After that, it's locked. And if you shared that Story to Facebook too, the edit option might not show up.

For me, this is huge on shoot days. When I'm covering an event and posting behind-the-scenes fast, mistakes happen. Now I've got a 15-minute safety net.

They also made it easier to post multiple photos in one Story, which is perfect for event recaps.

If you don't see it yet, update your app. It's rolling out slowly. Follow for more creator tips.

## TOPIC 1 — Lightroom Can Now Open Closed Eyes (Adobe MAX 2026)

### What Happened
At Adobe MAX 2026 in Miami Beach (Nov 10), Adobe rolled out a big batch of AI updates. For photographers the headline is Lightroom's new "Open Eyes" tool: it fixes closed eyes by using another photo of the same person from your library (you pick the reference or Lightroom finds one). It's in beta / early access, uses Generative Credits, and saves the fix as a new DNG. Lightroom also got "Glow" (Bloom, Diffusion, Halation, Motion, Streak), and Photoshop added Relight and Generative Restyle.

### Why It Matters
Closed eyes is the #1 group-shot killer at events, weddings and corporate shoots. Until now the fix was a manual head-swap in Photoshop. A one-click fix changes how much time event photographers spend in post — but it also raises the "is this still a real photo?" question, and it costs credits.

### Why It Suits Me
I shoot events for Pixel With Bliss — group photos and blinks are a real, everyday problem for me. I can speak to it as a working event photographer, not just someone reading the news.

### Hook
"Lightroom can now open your client's eyes — and I've got mixed feelings about it."

### Talking Points
- Open Eyes uses a different photo of the same person as a reference to fix blinks — it's in beta and uses Generative Credits.
- Lightroom also got Glow (halation/diffusion film-style effects), and Photoshop got Relight to move and add light after the shot.
- It saves time, but you still need to nail the shot — and be honest with clients about AI edits.

### My Angle
As an event photographer: this is a genuine time-saver for group shots, but it shouldn't replace good technique (shoot bursts, call "eyes open on three"). Raise the ethics question: is swapping eyes from another frame any different from the head-swaps we've done in Photoshop for years? Ask the audience where they draw the line.

### 30–60 Second Script
Lightroom can now open your client's eyes — and I've got mixed feelings about it.

So at Adobe MAX this week, Adobe announced a Lightroom feature called Open Eyes. If someone blinks in a photo, Lightroom grabs another shot of that same person from your library and uses it to fix the eyes. It's in beta right now, and it uses Generative Credits.

As someone who shoots events — this is huge. Group photo, twenty people, there's always that one person who blinks. Before, that meant a manual head swap in Photoshop.

They also added Glow — think film-style halation — and Photoshop got Relight, which lets you add and move light after the shot.

But here's my take: this doesn't replace good habits. I still shoot bursts and say "eyes open on three."

And honestly — is fixing a blink with the same person's real eyes cheating? Or is it just the new head swap? Let me know where you draw the line.

## TOPIC 2 — Claude Can Now Open Canva, Adobe & Notion Inside the Chat

### What Happened
On Nov 10, 2026, Anthropic launched "interactive connectors": supported apps can now open beside your Claude chat (or full screen) and Claude can make edits in them live — with your approval. Named launch apps include Canva, Adobe, Notion, Dropbox, Asana, Box, Atlassian, HubSpot and Webflow; Anthropic's example for Adobe is retouching/editing photos and editing PDFs. It's rolling out on web and desktop across all plans. Same day, Anthropic also launched Claude Sheets and Whiteboards (beta, paid plans) and previewed Claude Meetings.

### Why It Matters
Creators juggle 5+ tabs per post — script in one app, design in Canva, notes in Notion. Having the AI work inside those tools instead of just giving you text to copy-paste is a real workflow shift, and it's built on MCP, the same standard used by automation tools.

## TOPIC 3 — You Can Now Claim Your Business Inside ChatGPT

### What Happened
On Nov 10, 2026, OpenAI launched "Businesses in ChatGPT" in beta. Through a new Business Hub, verified businesses can claim their profile, update their details and product info, and see how often ChatGPT mentions them, website clicks, and topics they're cited for. Some U.S. e-commerce and online-service businesses can also build a "Business Agent" customers can chat with, and businesses can sponsor those agents as ads.

### Why It Matters
People are starting to ask ChatGPT for recommendations — "best event photographer in Toronto" — the way they used to ask Google. This is the start of "Google Business Profile, but for AI." Small businesses and creators who get in early can control what ChatGPT says about them.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm building Pixel With Bliss from zero and trying to land my first clients — how clients find small creative businesses is directly my problem. I can talk about it as a small business owner checking whether I can claim my own profile.

### Hook
"ChatGPT just became the new Google Business Profile — and most small businesses have no idea."

### Talking Points
- OpenAI's new Business Hub lets verified businesses claim their ChatGPT profile and update their info — it's in beta.
- You can see how often ChatGPT mentions you and how many clicks you get from it.
- Eligibility is still limited (some features U.S.-only), so check now — being early matters.

### My Angle
Small-business owner perspective: "Clients used to Google us — now they're asking AI." Don't overclaim ranking benefits (OpenAI hasn't said claiming boosts recommendations). Position it as: control your info, watch the data, and check eligibility — and do a follow-up showing whether a Toronto photography business like mine can claim yet.

### 30–60 Second Script
ChatGPT just became the new Google Business Profile — and most small businesses have no idea.

This week OpenAI launched something called Business Hub. If you own a business, you can verify it and claim your profile inside ChatGPT. You can update your info, and you can see how often ChatGPT mentions you and how many people click through to your website.

Why does this matter? Because people are starting to ask ChatGPT stuff like, "Who's a good event photographer in Toronto?" — instead of Googling it.

Now, to be clear — it's in beta, some features are U.S.-only right now, and OpenAI hasn't said claiming your profile gets you recommended more.

But as someone building a photography business from scratch, I'm checking if I can claim mine today. If you run a small business, you should too.

Want me to show you how it goes? Comment "profile."

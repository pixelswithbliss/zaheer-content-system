## TOPIC 1 — You Can Now Claim Your Business Inside ChatGPT

### What Happened
On November 10, 2026, OpenAI launched a beta called Business Hub as part of "Businesses in ChatGPT." Businesses can claim their ChatGPT profile, verify it (phone, business email, website domain or Shopify, plus an ID check through Persona), update the info ChatGPT shows, and see 28-day insights like mentions, website clicks and the topics people ask about. Eligible US online retailers and service businesses can also publish a "Business Agent" that answers customer questions. Claiming and the agent are free at launch.

### Why It Matters
More people are asking ChatGPT "who's a good photographer near me" instead of Googling. This is the first official way to control what ChatGPT says about your business and to see whether it's sending you clicks. Note the limits: local-business claims currently only cover existing US listings, and Business Agents are US-only at launch — so Canadian creators may not be able to fully claim yet.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a solo Toronto photographer trying to land first clients, and I'm learning AI tools. "How do clients find me?" is my real problem, so I can speak to this as a small-business owner, not a tech reporter.

### Hook
People are asking ChatGPT to recommend photographers now — and as of this week, businesses can finally claim their profile in it.

### Talking Points
- OpenAI's new Business Hub lets you claim, verify and edit how your business appears in ChatGPT — free for now.
- You get stats: how often ChatGPT mentions you and how many people click through to your site.
- It's US-first for local businesses, so outside the US, make sure your website and listings clearly say what you do and where — that's what AI pulls from.

### My Angle
AI search is becoming the new Google Maps for small creative businesses. I'm not in the US, so I can't fully use it yet — and that's the point: start cleaning up your website, services and location info now so you're ready when it rolls out wider. Ask the audience: "Have you ever asked ChatGPT to recommend a business?"

## TOPIC 2 — Getty vs Stability AI: The Photographers' AI Copyright Fight Is Back in Court

### What Happened
On November 11–12, 2026, Getty Images took its case against Stability AI (maker of Stable Diffusion) to the UK Court of Appeal in London. Last November the High Court mostly sided with Stability. Getty now argues the AI model itself can be an "infringing copy" even if it doesn't store the photos it was trained on. Stability argues the training happened on servers outside the UK, so UK copyright law doesn't apply. No ruling has been announced yet.

### Why It Matters
This is one of the biggest tests of whether training AI on photographers' work without permission is legal. The outcome could shape whether photographers ever get paid or protected when their images train AI models.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a working photographer — my portfolio is exactly the kind of content these models learn from. I can talk about how it feels from the creator side without pretending to be a lawyer.

### Hook
Can an AI company train on your photos in one country and get away with it in another? A UK court is deciding that right now.

### Talking Points
- Getty is appealing after losing most of its UK case against Stability AI last year.
- Getty's argument: the AI model itself counts as an illegal copy, even if no photos are stored inside it.
- Stability's argument: training happened outside the UK, so UK law doesn't apply — and no decision yet.

### My Angle
The "it happened on a server somewhere else" defence is the scary part for every photographer. If that works, borders become a loophole. Keep it balanced: no ruling yet, but photographers should watch this and think about where and how they post their work.

### 30–60 Second Script
Can an AI company train on your photos in one country — and get away with it in another? A UK court is literally deciding that right now.

So this week, Getty Images was back in court in London against Stability AI, the company behind Stable Diffusion. Last year, Getty mostly lost this case.

Now they're appealing, and the argument is pretty interesting. Getty says the AI model itself is basically an illegal copy of their photos — even if the actual images aren't stored inside it.

Stability's response? The training happened on computers outside the UK, so UK law doesn't apply.

As a photographer, that second part is what gets me. If "we did it on a server somewhere else" works as a defence… where does that leave the rest of us?

No ruling yet — I'll update you when there is one. Which side do you think wins?

## TOPIC 3 — RED Komodo-X Drops to $4,995

### What Happened
Around November 11–12, 2026, the RED KOMODO-X 6K cinema camera dropped $2,000 to $4,995 (body only) at B&H and CVP, in Nikon Z and Canon RF mounts. It launched in 2023 at $9,995 and was cut to $6,995 in 2024. It's labelled a holiday sale, with no announced end date. RED is now owned by Nikon.

### Why It Matters
It's a Super35 global-shutter camera shooting REDCODE RAW up to 6K 80p — real cinema-camera tech — now priced close to high-end hybrid mirrorless bodies. For filmmakers it reopens the "hybrid vs dedicated cinema camera" decision.

### Why It Suits Me
Cinematic film is one of my core focuses. "Would I actually buy this as a small creator?" is a genuine, relatable question from someone growing a business on the side.

### Hook
A RED cinema camera that launched at ten thousand dollars is now under five grand. But should a small creator actually buy one?

### Talking Points
- KOMODO-X is down $2,000 to $4,995 body-only, in Nikon Z or Canon RF mount — a holiday deal, so it may not last.
- Global shutter, 6K RAW up to 80fps — no rolling-shutter wobble on fast motion.
- But the body is just the start: batteries, media, monitor and rigging add up fast — and it's not a stills/hybrid camera.

### My Angle
Price drop ≠ smart buy. For a solo creator doing events plus short-form, a hybrid mirrorless body probably still makes more business sense. A cinema body makes sense once clients actually pay for that look. Frame it as "buy for your clients, not for the logo."

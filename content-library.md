## TOPIC 1 — Insta360 Hits 10 Million Cameras Sold, Opens First US Flagship Store in Times Square

### What Happened
Insta360 announced it has shipped 10 million cameras worldwide and opened its first flagship retail store outside Asia at 1515 Broadway in Times Square, NYC, on September 19, 2026. It follows a Tokyo flagship opened in August, with European expansion (Munich) planned next. Per IDC, Insta360 holds roughly 70% share of the global 360° camera market.

### Why It Matters
Insta360 dominates the 360°/action camera category that a huge share of short-form creators rely on for POV shots and dynamic B-roll. A brand milestone like this plus a move into physical retail signals the format is becoming mainstream gear, not a niche accessory.

### Why It Suits Me
I shoot events and media coverage for Pixel With Bliss, where action/360 cameras are common for behind-the-scenes and dynamic coverage — this is directly gear-relevant, and it's an easy, visual "brand milestone" story to react to on camera.

### Hook
"Insta360 just sold its 10 millionth camera — and opened a flagship store in Times Square to prove it."

### Talking Points
- 10 million cameras shipped worldwide, ~70% share of the global 360° camera market per IDC
- First flagship store outside Asia, in Times Square, following a Tokyo store opened in August
- Insta360 explicitly framed the store as a bet on "the American creator ecosystem"

### My Angle
Whether physical retail still matters for a camera brand in 2026 when most creator-gear buying decisions come from TikTok reviews — is this a marketing flex, or a real signal that action/360 cameras are becoming as mainstream as DSLRs once were?

### 30–60 Second Script
"Insta360 just hit a massive milestone — 10 million cameras sold worldwide. And instead of just posting about it, they opened a full flagship store in Times Square. Not Tokyo, not Shenzhen — Times Square, New York. That's a huge statement. Insta360 already owns close to 70% of the global 360 camera market, so this isn't really about needing more sales. It's about legitimacy — they want to be seen as a real camera brand, not just 'that action cam,' right next to the big electronics stores. As someone who shoots events and content for a living, I think this says a lot about where the industry's heading. Action and 360 cameras used to be a fun add-on. Now they're becoming a core part of how creators capture stuff, because footage from these things just performs better on Reels and Shorts. Honestly, if you're a content creator and you don't have one of these in your kit yet, this might be your sign."

## TOPIC 2 — Canon Officially Announces the EOS R8 Mark II

### What Happened
Canon officially announced the EOS R8 Mark II full-frame mirrorless camera and a new Speedlite EL-5 Mark II flash on September 15, 2026. It's Canon's lightest EOS body with in-body image stabilization, packs a 24.2MP full-frame sensor, shoots up to 40fps with the electronic shutter, and offers a much deeper buffer — 58% more uninterrupted JPEG shots and 30% more RAW shots than the original R8.

### Why It Matters
The R8 line is Canon's entry point into full-frame with IBIS — the camera many creators and photographers upgrade to when they outgrow crop-sensor bodies. Deeper buffer and faster bursts directly affect anyone shooting fast-moving events, sports, or kids/pets content.

### Why It Suits Me
I shoot events for Pixel With Bliss, where buffer depth and burst speed are exactly the practical specs that matter on the floor at a wedding or event — a natural, credible gear-talk topic for me.

### Hook
"Canon just fixed the biggest complaint about the R8 — and it's official as of this week."

### Talking Points
- Lightest Canon full-frame body ever with in-body stabilization
- 40fps burst with a much deeper buffer — 58% more JPEGs, 30% more RAW before it slows down
- Announced alongside a new flash, the Speedlite EL-5 Mark II

### My Angle
Compare this to real event-shooting experience — buffer depth is an unsexy spec nobody talks about, but it's the difference between catching the key moment or missing it because the camera locked up mid-burst. Give an honest take on whether this makes the R8 line viable for event work.

### 30–60 Second Script
"Canon just announced the EOS R8 Mark II, and honestly the spec that jumps out at me isn't the megapixels — it's the buffer. They're saying 58% more JPEGs and 30% more RAW files before the camera slows down on burst. If you've ever shot a wedding or an event, you know exactly why that matters — it's always the moment you need five extra frames where the buffer decides to fill up. It's also the lightest full-frame Canon with in-body stabilization, so it's clearly built for people who are on their feet all day, like event shooters. They dropped a new flash alongside it too, the EL-5 Mark II. For anyone who's been eyeing the R8 as a lightweight second body or a starter full-frame setup, this update actually addresses the real complaint people had. ## TOPIC 3 — Capture One's AI Culling Tool Now Runs Live During Tethered Shoots

### What Happened
Capture One shipped an update (reported September 16, 2026) that makes its AI-powered "Assisted Review" culling tool run automatically during tethered shoots instead of requiring a manual trigger, flagging images with closed eyes, missed focus, or exposure problems in real time. The same update expands 2nd-gen wireless tethering support to the Canon EOS R3 and EOS R6 III. The culling tool remains in beta and desktop-only.

### Why It Matters
Culling is one of the most time-consuming parts of a photographer's workflow, especially after a big shoot. Automating it during capture — not after — means photographers could walk away from a shoot with selects nearly done.

### Why It Suits Me
This is squarely in my niche — AI tools for photographers — and it directly affects post-production time on the event and portrait work that's the backbone of Pixel With Bliss.

### Hook
"Your camera software might start culling your photos for you — while you're still shooting."

### Talking Points
- Capture One's AI culling tool (Assisted Review) now runs live during tethered shoots, not just after
- It flags closed eyes, missed focus, and exposure problems in real time
- Same update adds wireless tethering support for the Canon EOS R3 and EOS R6 III

### My Angle
A practical, slightly skeptical take — genuinely useful for studio/tethered work, but most event photographers (including me) aren't shooting tethered. What would it take for this kind of AI culling to reach handheld event shooters, and why that's the tool I actually want next.

### 30–60 Second Script
"Capture One just pushed an update where their AI culling tool now runs live, while you're shooting tethered. So instead of finishing a shoot and then spending an hour going through hundreds of photos for closed eyes or missed focus, the software's already flagging the bad ones as you go. That's a big deal for studio photographers. My honest take, though — most event photographers like me aren't shooting tethered, we're moving around a room with a camera in hand. So the tool I actually want is this same kind of AI culling, but built for handheld shooting, syncing after the fact. Still, this is a sign of where things are heading — AI doing the boring grunt work of photography so we can spend more time actually shooting and editing the photos that matter. If you shoot tethered at all — portraits, product, studio work — this is worth checking out even in beta."

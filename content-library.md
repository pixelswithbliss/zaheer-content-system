## TOPIC 1 — Affinity for iPad is now free

### What Happened
Canva released a new all-in-one Affinity app for iPad around Nov 2-3, 2026. It combines photo editing, vector design and layout in one app, supports RAW, and the core tools are free with a free Canva account. AI features need a paid Canva plan. Some desktop tools like batch processing and macros are missing for now and are promised in later updates.

### Why It Matters
A free, real photo editor on iPad means anyone can edit RAW photos without paying Adobe every month. It lowers the cost of starting out as a photographer.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a photographer with Pixel With Bliss, I care about editing tools, and I'm building this with no revenue yet, so free software is a real topic for me.

### Hook
Adobe's biggest competitor just made its iPad photo editor free.

### Talking Points
- It's one app for photo editing, vector and layout, and it handles RAW.
- Core tools are free. AI features cost extra, and batch editing and macros aren't there yet.
- It's great for editing on the go, but not a full replacement for a Lightroom workflow yet.

### My Angle
Be honest as a working photographer: free is great for beginners and quick edits, but missing batch processing matters if you shoot events and deliver hundreds of photos. Say what you'd actually use it for.

### 30–60 Second Script
Okay, this one is actually big for photographers. Canva just released the Affinity app for iPad, and the main tools are free. It's one app: photo editing, design, layout, and it opens RAW files. You just need a free Canva account. Now, the AI stuff costs money, and a few things are missing right now, like batch editing. And if you shoot events like I do, batch editing is a big deal. So I'm not dumping my whole workflow for it. But if you're starting out and can't afford a subscription, this is a really good place to start. Free is hard to argue with. Would you try it? Let me know.

## TOPIC 2 — ChatGPT can now make GIFs

### What Happened
On Nov 4, 2026, OpenAI released Images 2.6. It makes images about 50% faster and lets ChatGPT create animated GIFs from a text prompt or an uploaded photo. You can change the speed and edit single frames. It's rolling out to ChatGPT and Codex users on web, desktop and mobile.

### Why It Matters
Creators can make quick animated visuals for posts, stories and video overlays without any animation skills.

### Why It Suits Me
I cover AI tools for creators and I'm learning automation. This is a simple tool I can test on camera.

### Hook
ChatGPT can now turn your photo into a GIF, so I tested what it's actually good for.

### Talking Points
- Prompt or photo in, GIF out, with speed and frame editing.
- Rolling out gradually, so you may not see it yet.
- Good for fun overlays and memes. It's not a replacement for real video.

### My Angle
Don't hype it. Test it with one of my own photos and show the real result, good or bad. Then say whether it's usable for client work. Mention that I haven't tested it yet if I haven't.

### 30–60 Second Script
ChatGPT can now make GIFs. OpenAI released a new image model called Images 2.6, and you can type a prompt, or upload a photo, and it gives you an animated GIF. You can change the speed and even edit frame by frame. It's rolling out slowly, so you might not have it yet. Is this going to replace real video? No. But for quick overlays, stickers, or little animated bits for your posts, it's a fast way to get something. I'm going to try it with one of my own photos and show you the result. If it's bad, I'll tell you. Follow so you see the test.

## TOPIC 3 — Instagram wants YouTube's long videos

### What Happened
Instagram started a US test on Oct 26, 2026 of "Features", horizontal videos up to 90 minutes, for select creators. Instagram hasn't announced an ad revenue share for it. Reports on Nov 2 say some YouTube creators were offered large payments to post there. The payment claim comes from news reports, not an Instagram announcement.

### Why It Matters
It shows Instagram trying to compete with YouTube for long-form video. Creators should know before moving anything.

### Why It Suits Me
I post short-form and reserve Instagram for my portfolio. This affects where I put long content later.

### Hook
Instagram now lets some creators post 90-minute videos, and it's coming for YouTube.

### Talking Points
- It's a limited test, not open to everyone.
- No announced ad revenue share, and the payment offers are reported, not confirmed by Instagram.
- For a small creator, YouTube is still where long-form earns.

### My Angle
As a small creator, I'm not rebuilding around a test. I'd watch whether Instagram adds real monetization before spending time there.

### 30–60 Second Script
Instagram is testing videos up to 90 minutes long. It's called Features, and right now only select creators in the US have it. Basically, Instagram wants a piece of YouTube. And reports say they've offered some YouTubers big money to try it, though Instagram hasn't confirmed that. The thing creators are asking is, how do you get paid? Right now there's no announced ad revenue share. So for me? I'm not changing anything. YouTube pays creators. Instagram is still a test. I'll watch it, and if they add real monetization, I'll talk about it again.

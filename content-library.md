## TOPIC 1 — Instagram Finally Lets You Edit Stories After Posting (But Only for 15 Minutes)

### What Happened
Instagram now lets you edit a Story after you've posted it. For 15 minutes after sharing, you can change the text, music, stickers and drawings without deleting it, and you keep your views, likes and replies. Adam Mosseri confirmed the change on November 18, 2026, and edited Stories show an "Edited" label.

### Why It Matters
Before this, one typo meant deleting the Story and reposting — and losing every view you'd already earned. For creators and small businesses posting promos, event recaps or client shoutouts, that's a real fix. But the 15-minute window means you still need to check your Story right after posting. Reports (PCMag via BusinessToday) also say the button may not show up for Stories shared to Facebook or ones using certain stickers like "Add Yours."

### Why It Suits Me
I post event coverage and behind-the-scenes Stories for Pixel With Bliss — tagging clients, venues and adding music. This is a practical "here's what changed and how to use it" tip from someone who actually posts Stories for business.

### Hook
"Instagram just fixed the most annoying thing about Stories — but you've only got 15 minutes."

### Talking Points
- You can now edit text, music, stickers and drawings on a Story for 15 minutes after posting — and keep your views, likes and replies.
- Tap the three dots on your live Story and hit "Edit Story." Edited Stories get an "Edited" label.
- The catch: after 15 minutes it's locked, and the option may not appear if you shared to Facebook too or used stickers like "Add Yours."

### My Angle
Treat the 15 minutes as a built-in "quality check" habit: post, then immediately watch your own Story back on your phone like a viewer would — check the tag, the spelling of the client's name, the music. For anyone doing business on Instagram, a misspelled client tag looks worse than a late post.

### 30–60 Second Script
Instagram just fixed the most annoying thing about Stories — but you've only got 15 minutes.

So you post a Story, and right after, you notice the typo. Or you tagged the wrong account. Before, your only option was delete it and repost — and lose every view you already had.

Now, Instagram lets you edit your Story after posting. You can change the text, the music, stickers and drawings — and you keep your views, likes and replies. Just tap the three dots on your Story and hit "Edit Story." Adam Mosseri confirmed it this week.

Here's the catch. You only get 15 minutes. After that, it's locked. And it might not show up if you also shared it to Facebook or used certain stickers like "Add Yours."

So here's my tip as someone who posts event Stories for clients: right after you post, watch your own Story like a viewer. Check the client's name, the tag, the song. You've got 15 minutes — use them.

## TOPIC 3 — Panasonic's New LUMIX SHX1 Records Blackmagic RAW — Big News for DaVinci Resolve Users

### What Happened
On November 17, 2026, Panasonic announced the LUMIX SHX1, its first compact "box-style" cinema camera. It has a 6K full-frame 24.1MP partially stacked sensor, records Blackmagic RAW straight to an external USB-C SSD, records ProRes RAW internally to CFexpress Type B, and comes with ARRI LogC3 pre-installed alongside V-Log. US pricing is about $4,699.99 body-only ($5,499.99 kit), with availability rolling out in stages from December 2026.

### Why It Matters
Seeing Blackmagic RAW in a Panasonic camera is unusual — it's normally associated with Blackmagic's own cameras — and BRAW is built to work smoothly in DaVinci Resolve. Add in-body stabilization, phase-hybrid autofocus and ARRI LogC3, and this is a box camera aimed at solo shooters and small crews, not just big productions.

### Why It Suits Me
I edit everything in DaVinci Resolve and shoot cinematic event content. I can speak to what BRAW-to-SSD actually means in an editing workflow, and whether a ~$4,700 box camera makes sense for a solo creator vs. a regular mirrorless camera.

### Hook
"Panasonic just made a camera that records Blackmagic RAW — and if you edit in DaVinci Resolve, that's a big deal."

### Talking Points
- 6K full-frame box cinema camera, ~US$4,699.99 body, shipping in stages from December 2026.
- Records Blackmagic RAW to a USB-C SSD and ProRes RAW internally; ARRI LogC3 + V-Log built in.
- Has in-body stabilization and phase-hybrid AF — rare for a box camera, so it could actually work for solo shooters.

### My Angle
Honest solo-creator take: this is exciting, but it's a box camera — you'll need a monitor, handle, power and SSD to make it a real rig, so the true cost is higher than the sticker price. For most people starting out (like me), a good mirrorless camera still makes more sense. But the bigger story is the trend: camera brands are building for Resolve-based workflows, and that's a win for every Resolve editor.

Panasonic just made a camera that records Blackmagic RAW — and if you edit in DaVinci Resolve, that's a big deal.

It's called the LUMIX SHX1. It's Panasonic's first compact cinema camera — a little box with a 6K full-frame sensor.

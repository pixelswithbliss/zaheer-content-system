## TOPIC 1 — Adobe Just Let AI Agents Work Inside Your Photoshop and Premiere Files

### What Happened
At Adobe MAX on November 10, 2026, Adobe announced a local MCP integration (in preview) for Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere and After Effects. It lets third-party AI agents, including chatbots like ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, work on your actual project files, and Adobe says layers, sequences and compositions stay editable. Adobe also announced "always-on" background agents (preview) for repetitive jobs like organizing assets, a new Premiere color grading system called Color Mode, Photoshop Relight, and a Firefly Video tool that assembles videos from clips, images and sound effects.

### Why It Matters
Until now, most AI editing has spit out a flat, finished result. This is about AI touching your real working file and leaving it editable, so you can still fix it by hand. For photographers and video editors, that could mean handing off boring jobs (resizing, renaming, sorting, prepping exports) while keeping control of the creative decisions. It's also a sign the big creative apps are opening their doors to outside AI, not only Adobe's own.

### Why It Suits Me
I shoot events and edit video, and I'm learning AI automation (n8n, Claude, ChatGPT). This sits right where those two worlds meet. I can talk about it as someone who would actually use it to save hours on event deliverables, not as a tech reviewer.

### Hook
"Adobe just made it possible for ChatGPT or Claude to work inside your Photoshop file, and keep every layer editable."

### Talking Points

- Adobe also showed always-on background agents (preview) for repetitive tasks like organizing assets, plus Premiere Color Mode and Photoshop Relight.
- The real win isn't "AI makes art". It's AI taking over the boring admin parts of editing so creators can focus on the creative calls.

### My Angle

I'm not excited about AI replacing my edit; I'm excited about AI doing the renaming, resizing and exporting at 2am while I sleep. But it's a preview, so I'd be honest: I'll test it before I trust it with client work. And the photographers who learn to direct these tools will move faster than the ones who ignore them.

## TOPIC 2 — VSCO Says It Won't Train AI on Your Photos, Even If You're a Free User

### What Happened
VSCO updated its Creator Content Standards, effective November 10, 2026. The policy now says public or private creator content won't be used to train generative AI, for paid members and free users alike. Before this, VSCO's standards said free users' public content could be used for generative AI training, and only paying members were fully excluded. Partners also have to agree in their contracts not to train generative AI on public creator content.

### Why It Matters
Photographers have been nervous about where their photos end up as AI training data. A major photo app putting a clear "no generative AI training" line in its policy, for free users too, raises the bar for other platforms. One caveat matters: VSCO can still use public content for non-generative AI, like recommendations and moderation.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a photographer posting my portfolio online, so who can use my images is a real concern for me and for my audience. It's a simple, relatable story I can explain in under a minute, and it starts a good debate.

### Hook
"One photo app just promised it won't use your photos to train AI, even if you never paid them a cent."

### Talking Points
- VSCO's updated Creator Content Standards (effective Nov 10, 2026) say public or private content won't be used to train generative AI, for both paid and free users.
- Before, free users' public photos could be used for generative AI training. Now that's off the table, and partners must also agree not to train on public content.
- Read the fine print: public content can still be used for non-generative AI like recommendations and moderation.

### My Angle
I'll give credit where it's due, but stay realistic: a policy is a promise, not a lock. Photographers should actually read the terms of every app they upload to, and this should be the standard everywhere, not a bonus. Then ask the audience: does an app's AI policy decide where you post your work?

### 30–60 Second Script

As a photographer, I'll give them credit. But honestly, this should be the standard on every platform, not a bonus. And a policy is a promise, so read the terms of every app you upload your work to.

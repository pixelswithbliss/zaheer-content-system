## TOPIC 1 — The New York Times Just Told Its Photographers: Don't Use the New Photoshop

### What Happened
On November 24, 2026, The New York Times confirmed it told its contributing photographers to avoid Adobe Photoshop 2027 (version 28.0.0 and later) when working on assignment. A Times spokesperson said certain functions in Photoshop 2027 "utilize generative AI in ways that may not be apparent to users." Using them can add metadata that flags a photo as AI-edited, and the Times can't publish photos labelled that way. Photographers can roll back to Photoshop 27.0 through the Creative Cloud app. Adobe had not responded to PetaPixel when the story ran.

### Why It Matters
Your editing software can now mark your work as "AI-edited" even if you didn't mean to use AI. For anyone selling real photos (journalists, event shooters, wedding photographers), a label like that can affect trust and whether a client accepts your images. It also shows that big clients are starting to set rules about which software and which tools you're allowed to use.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm a working event photographer who delivers real moments to clients, so whether my edits look "AI-generated" matters to me. I edit photos every week, and I can talk about this as a working creator instead of just reading out the headline.

### Hook
"The New York Times just told its photographers to stop using the newest version of Photoshop. Here's why."

### Talking Points
- The NYT told contributing photographers to avoid Photoshop 2027 and roll back to 27.0, because some features use generative AI in ways that may not be obvious.
- The real problem is the metadata: using those tools can label a photo as AI-edited, and the Times can't run photos labelled that way.
- The Times still supports Content Credentials (C2PA). The issue is a lack of clarity, not the idea of labelling itself.

### My Angle
I don't think this is "AI is bad." I think it's about knowing your tools. As a photographer you need to know exactly which buttons add AI and which don't, because your clients will start asking. I'd like a clear "GenAI off" switch in Photoshop. And I'd tell other photographers that if you shoot events or anything documentary, you should be able to explain your edits to a client.

## TOPIC 1 — Lightroom Can Now Open Closed Eyes. Should Photographers Use It?

### What Happened
At Adobe MAX on November 10, 2026, Adobe rolled out a batch of AI editing tools for photographers. Lightroom's "Open Eyes" (in beta) fixes a blink by borrowing the eyes from another photo of the same person in your library. You can pick that photo yourself or let Lightroom find it. Photoshop also got "Relight", which lets you add new light sources to a finished photo and move them around, and Lightroom got a new "Glow" effect. Adobe did not raise Photography plan prices, but the newer partner AI models inside Firefly use up generative credits faster.

### Why It Matters
Blinks ruin group shots and candid event photos all the time. A tool that fixes them in seconds could save a lot of keepers. But it also raises a real question: when does editing turn into making things up? Clients, couples and event organizers trust that what they see actually happened. And because the heavier AI features cost credits, people on the cheaper plans could run out quickly.

### Why It Suits Me
I shoot events, and closed eyes in group photos is one of the most common problems in event coverage. I use editing tools every day, so I can speak from real experience about where I'd use this and where I'd draw the line. It also connects to my interest in AI tools without being hype.

### Hook
"Lightroom can now open someone's closed eyes in your photo, and I'm not sure how I feel about that."

### Talking Points
- Open Eyes doesn't make up new eyes. It borrows them from another photo of the same person, and it's still in beta.
- Photoshop's new Relight lets you add and move light after the shoot. That's useful, but it's not a replacement for lighting well on the day.
- No price increase, but watch your generative credits. Some of the newer AI models inside Firefly use several credits each time.

### My Angle
As an event photographer, I'd use it for group shots where one blink ruins a great moment, since that person really was there with their eyes open seconds before. I wouldn't use it to change what actually happened. Ask your audience where they draw the line, and say that being upfront with clients about AI edits matters more now than ever.

## TOPIC 2 — YouTube Will Now Turn Your Long Videos Into Shorts For You

### What Happened
On November 12, 2026, YouTube announced that the video clips tool in YouTube Studio can now make vertical Shorts from your long-form videos, starting with podcast episodes. You pick a section from the transcript or timeline, and YouTube reframes the 16:9 footage to vertical, adds automatic captions you can edit, and links the Short back to the full video. AI-suggested clip moments are available in 10 countries, including Canada and the US. The manual tool works everywhere.

### Why It Matters
Repurposing long videos into Shorts usually means re-editing in another app. Now it can happen inside YouTube Studio, and the automatic link back to the full video can send Shorts viewers to your long-form content. YouTube's help page also says you won't be penalized for clipping your own content.

### Why It Suits Me
I'm building on YouTube and editing in DaVinci Resolve, so I can compare a one-click tool with doing it by hand. It's a practical, useful tip for small creators and businesses in Canada who don't have time to edit every clip.

### Hook
"If you post long videos on YouTube, YouTube will now cut your Shorts for you."

### Talking Points
- In YouTube Studio's clips tool, pick a section of your long video and it becomes a vertical Short with captions.
- The Short automatically links back to the full video. That's free traffic to your long-form content.
- AI picks the best moments for you in Canada, the US and 8 other countries, but the auto-crop won't always frame things the way you want.

### My Angle
Speed versus quality. It's great for getting more Shorts out of what you already made, but a lazy auto-crop can look obviously cut down. My take: use it for volume, but hand-edit your best moments in Resolve so they actually hook people. Shorts that are made for vertical still beat Shorts that were just reframed.

### 30–60 Second Script
If you post long videos on YouTube, YouTube will now cut your Shorts for you.

YouTube just updated the clips tool inside YouTube Studio. You pick a section from one of your long videos, either from the timeline or by highlighting part of the transcript, and it turns it into a vertical Short. It reframes the shot, adds captions you can edit, and here's the best part: the Short automatically links back to your full video.

And if you're here in Canada, it can even suggest which moments to clip using AI.

Now, my honest take. As someone who edits everything in DaVinci Resolve, an auto-crop is never going to be as good as a Short you actually designed for vertical. But if you're a small creator or a business with a pile of long videos just sitting there, this is free reach you're leaving on the table.

So use it for volume, and hand-edit your best moments.

## TOPIC 1 — Instagram Wants YouTubers: 90-Minute Videos and Up to $500K

### What Happened
On Oct 26, Instagram announced it's testing "Features," a video upload type that allows videos up to 90 minutes long, plus "Clips as Reels," which cuts pieces of a long video into Reels that link back to the full video. Testing started in Brazil and is expanding to a small US test. Reports on Oct 29-30 say Instagram is offering some YouTubers up to $500,000 to post in the format. Meta says it's too early to share a long-term monetization model.

### Why It Matters
Instagram is going after YouTube's long-form audience. Creators who only make Shorts/Reels may soon need a long-form plan, and nobody knows yet how Features will pay.

### Why It Suits Me
It's a platform change that affects every creator, and I'm building on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube at once. I can give the small-creator view that big headlines skip.

### Hook
Instagram just said it will let you post a 90-minute video, and it's reportedly paying YouTubers up to half a million dollars to try it.

### Talking Points
- Features: up to 90 minutes, testing in Brazil, small US test next
- Clips as Reels turns long videos into Reels pointing to the full video
- Big money for big YouTubers, but no monetization model for everyone else yet

### My Angle
Don't chase the money headline. For a small creator with no audience on a new format, the real question is whether this gives you a way to show real work (event recaps, behind-the-scenes edits) to people already on Instagram. Test it, don't rebuild around it.

### 30–60 Second Script
Instagram just announced something big for creators. It's testing a new video type called Features, and videos can be up to 90 minutes long. Yes, ninety. That's basically a YouTube video inside Instagram. It also has a tool that cuts pieces of your long video into Reels that link back to the full thing. Right now it's testing in Brazil, and it's coming to a small test in the US. And reports say Instagram is offering some YouTubers up to five hundred thousand dollars to post in this format. But Meta also said it's too early to say how everyone else will get paid. So here's my take. If you're small like me, don't rebuild everything around this yet. But a long video of a full event recap or a behind-the-scenes edit, with the Reel clips pointing to it? That could be really useful. I'll be watching this one. Would you post long-form on Instagram?

## TOPIC 2 — Nikon's $14,000 Lens With a Built-In Teleconverter

### What Happened
On Oct 29, 2026 Nikon officially announced the Nikkor Z 120-300mm f/2.8 TC VR S. It's a constant f/2.8 zoom with a built-in 1.4x teleconverter that extends it to 420mm (at f/4). US price is $13,999.95, with shipping expected mid-November. Nikon first announced it was in development in May 2026.

### Why It Matters
It's aimed at pro sports and wildlife shooters who want one lens instead of swapping teleconverters. The price shows where pro glass is going.

### Why It Suits Me
It's gear news with a debate in it: is a lens like this worth it, and what does it mean for people shooting on a budget? I can talk as a working event shooter who has to be realistic about gear.

### Hook
Nikon just launched a lens that costs almost fourteen thousand dollars, and it has a teleconverter built in.

### Talking Points
- 120-300mm f/2.8, built-in 1.4x teleconverter to 420mm f/4, about 2.7 kg
- US price $13,999.95, shipping mid-November
- Who it's for, and what a budget shooter should do instead

### My Angle
Most photographers will never need this. The better lesson is renting for the job and billing it to the client, instead of buying gear before you have the clients.

### 30–60 Second Script
Nikon just announced a lens that costs almost fourteen thousand dollars. It's the Nikkor Z 120-300 f/2.8. The cool part is it has a 1.4x teleconverter built right in, so you flip it on and you're at 420 millimeters. You lose a stop of light, so it goes to f/4, but you don't have to swap anything mid-game. It weighs around six pounds and ships in mid-November. That's for pro sports and wildlife shooters. For most of us, honestly, it's not the lens to buy. If you're starting out like me, rent gear for the job and build it into your price. Buy when the clients are already paying for it. Would you spend fourteen grand on a lens? Tell me below.

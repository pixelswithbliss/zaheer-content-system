## TOPIC 1 — TikTok Can Now Spot Deepfakes of You (Sort Of)

### What Happened
On November 20, 2026, TikTok announced at a London event that it's rolling out its "Likeness Detection" tool globally, gradually. It's opt-in: eligible users verify their ID, then TikTok alerts them when videos or accounts may be using their face, and they can report it. Right now it's aimed at public figures — politicians and accounts with big followings. TikTok also launched a free "Identify AI-Generated Content" checker (per Euronews) where anyone can upload an image or video to see if it was made with TikTok's or ByteDance's AI tools like CapCut or Dreamina.

### Why It Matters
Anyone who puts their face online is a potential deepfake target. Platforms are starting to build tools that let you find fakes of yourself instead of hoping someone tells you. But the checker only detects content from TikTok/ByteDance tools — it's not a universal AI detector, and the likeness tool isn't open to everyone yet.

### Why It Suits Me
I put my face on camera every day and I work with AI tools for editing. As a photographer, image authenticity is literally my business. I can explain this from both sides: someone who uses AI and someone whose face could be misused.

### Hook
"TikTok just launched a tool that can find deepfakes of your face — but there's a catch most people are going to miss."

### Talking Points
- Likeness Detection is rolling out globally, opt-in, with ID verification — but it's aimed at public figures and big accounts first.
- The free AI checker only catches stuff made with TikTok/ByteDance tools like CapCut — not every AI generator out there.
- Real takeaway: platforms are admitting deepfakes are a real problem, and creators need to start thinking about protecting their likeness.

### My Angle
Good first step, but don't treat it like a shield. If the checker says "not AI," that doesn't mean it's real — it just means it wasn't made with TikTok's own tools. For small creators, the best protection is still building a recognizable, consistent presence so your audience knows the real you.

So yesterday TikTok announced it's rolling out something called Likeness Detection around the world. You verify your ID, and TikTok will flag videos or accounts that might be using your face — then you can report them. Right now, though, it's aimed at public figures: politicians and accounts with big followings. It's rolling out gradually.

They also dropped a free checker where anyone can upload a photo or video and see if it was made with AI. Sounds great, right? Here's the catch — according to the reporting, it only detects content made with TikTok's own tools and ByteDance apps like CapCut. So if it says "not AI"… that doesn't mean it's real.

As someone who's on camera and works with photos every day, I think this is a good first step. But don't treat it like a shield. Pay attention, question what you see, and if you're a creator — start thinking about your face as something worth protecting.

## TOPIC 2 — You Can Finally Edit Instagram Stories After Posting

### What Happened
Instagram head Adam Mosseri announced that you can now edit a Story after posting it — for up to 15 minutes. You can change the text, music, stickers and drawings, and you keep your views, likes and replies. Edited Stories get an "edited" label, and you can't swap out the actual photo or video. Announced November 18, 2026, rolling out now.

### Why It Matters
Typos and wrong tags used to mean deleting the Story and losing all your early views. For creators and businesses posting promos, event info or client tags, that 15-minute window saves reach.

### Why It Suits Me
I use Instagram for my Pixel With Bliss event portfolio and post Stories from shoots — wrong tags, wrong dates, wrong music happen. It's a practical, quick tip I can demo.

### Hook
"If you've ever deleted an Instagram Story because of one typo — you don't have to anymore. But you've only got 15 minutes."

### Talking Points
- You can edit text, music, stickers and drawings for 15 minutes after posting — and keep your views, likes and replies.
- You can't change the actual photo or video, and viewers see an "edited" label.
- How to do it: open your Story, tap the three dots, hit "Edit Story."

### My Angle
Practical workflow tip: post, then immediately re-check your Story within the 15 minutes — especially client tags and event details. Little things like a wrong tag on a client's Story cost you the shoutout that brings in your next booking.

### 30–60 Second Script

Small update, but if you post for a business, it actually matters. Save this so you remember the 15-minute rule.

## TOPIC 3 — Panasonic's New LUMIX SHX1: A Cinema Camera for Indie Creators?

### What Happened
On November 17, 2026, Panasonic announced the LUMIX SHX1, its first compact cinema camera under the LUMIX name. It's a box-style full-frame camera with a 6K partially stacked sensor, internal ProRes RAW, Blackmagic RAW recording straight to an external USB-C SSD, ARRI LogC3 and V-Log built in, 32-bit float audio, in-body stabilization and phase-detect autofocus. It ships in stages from December 2026, around US$4,699 (per RedShark News; PetaPixel lists $4,697).

### Why It Matters
Features that used to require an external recorder or a much pricier cinema body are being packed into a box camera under $5K. Blackmagic RAW to an SSD is a big deal for anyone editing in DaVinci Resolve.

### Why It Suits Me
I shoot cinematic content and edit in DaVinci Resolve — Blackmagic RAW is native to my workflow. I can talk about whether a camera like this actually makes sense for a small, growing video business.

### Hook
"Panasonic just made a cinema camera that records Blackmagic RAW straight to an SSD — and as a DaVinci Resolve editor, that got my attention."

### Talking Points
- What it is: full-frame 6K box cinema camera, ARRI LogC3 + V-Log, 32-bit float audio, IBIS and autofocus.
- The workflow win: Blackmagic RAW to a USB-C SSD and internal ProRes RAW — no external recorder.
- The real question: at around $4,700 body-only, is this smart for a solo creator, or do skills and lenses matter more?

### My Angle
Exciting, but gear doesn't book clients. For someone at my stage, the lesson isn't "buy this" — it's that pro cinema workflows are getting cheaper and more accessible every year, so learning color grading and RAW workflows now will pay off.

### 30–60 Second Script

## TOPIC 1 — Sigma's $799 50-120mm f/2.8 Zoom for APS-C

### What Happened
On October 8, 2026 Sigma announced the 50-120mm F2.8 DC OS Contemporary, a telephoto zoom for APS-C cameras (about 75-180mm in full-frame terms). It comes in Sony E, Fujifilm X and Canon RF mounts, costs $799 USD and goes on sale October 22. It weighs about 390g and has 6.5 stops of stabilization at the wide end. There is no Nikon Z version.

### Why It Matters
A constant f/2.8 telephoto zoom at this price and size is rare for APS-C. It's relevant to anyone shooting events, portraits or video on a crop-sensor body without carrying a heavy full-frame setup.

### Why It Suits Me
I cover events and make content on a budget. Affordable, light gear that does real work is exactly what my audience of starting creators cares about.

### Hook
This $799 lens might be the best budget event lens for crop-sensor cameras, and Nikon shooters are left out.

### Talking Points
- 50-120mm f/2.8 on APS-C, roughly 75-180mm equivalent, 390g, stabilized
- $799, available October 22, for Sony E, Fujifilm X and Canon RF (no Nikon Z)
- It's a perfect fit for events, portraits and talking-head video at a lower price than most pro f/2.8 zooms

### My Angle
Spec sheets don't book clients. Ask whether an affordable f/2.8 zoom beats saving for full frame when you're starting out and need to land your first paid events. (I haven't tested it, so frame it as a reaction, not a review.)

### 30–60 Second Script
Sigma just announced a new lens, and if you shoot on a crop-sensor camera, pay attention. It's the 50-120mm f/2.8, and it costs $799. On a crop body that's about a 75 to 180 millimetre lens, with a constant f/2.8, and it weighs under 400 grams. It comes out October 22 for Sony E, Fujifilm X and Canon RF. Sorry Nikon Z shooters, it's not coming to you yet. For events and portraits, that range is exactly what you reach for. Now, I haven't tested it, so this isn't a review. Let me know what you think in the comments.

## TOPIC 2 — Facebook Is Testing a Reels-First App

### What Happened
Meta is testing a version of Facebook that opens to a full-screen Reels feed instead of the traditional News Feed for some users in India. A Meta spokesperson confirmed to TechCrunch that a test is also running in Indonesia. People in the test can opt out, and the classic feed stays as a second tab. Social Media Today also reports Instagram is testing letting users set Reels as their default home. Meta has said it will explore bringing this to the U.S. next year.

### Why It Matters
If Facebook opens to Reels, short video becomes even more of the way people discover creators there. It's still only a test, so nothing changes for most people today.

### Why It Suits Me
I post Facebook Reels. This is a signal about where Facebook is going.

### Hook
Facebook may soon open straight to Reels, and if you make short videos, that's good news.

### Talking Points
- Test for some users in India (and Indonesia), with an option to switch back
- Instagram is testing a similar open-to-Reels option
- US rollout is only being explored for next year, so don't over-react

### My Angle
Don't rebuild everything for a test. But this is another sign that short vertical video is the main way platforms want people to find you. Keep posting talking-head Reels on Facebook now.

### 30–60 Second Script
Facebook is testing something big. Some users in India and Indonesia now open the app and land straight on a full-screen Reels feed, not the normal News Feed. They can switch back, and the old feed is still there as a tab. Meta says it's looking at bringing this to the U.S. next year, so this is a test, not a change for everyone. Instagram is also testing letting people open straight to Reels. For us, the message is clear: short vertical video is where the platforms are heading. If you're a creator and you've been ignoring Facebook Reels, now's a good time to start posting there. I'm going to keep making Reels on all of my platforms. Are you?

OpenAI says GPT-6 Instant starts answering web-search questions 44% faster than GPT-5.6 Instant, which is OpenAI's own figure.
